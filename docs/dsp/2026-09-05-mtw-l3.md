# Multi-time-window transfer function — decisions (lane L3)

*2026-09-05. Lane L3, station 2. Written from the station-1 research pass
(three read-only agents: literature and commercial practice, open-source code,
this repo's own plug-in surface). Nothing in `core/` implements any of this yet.
The record exists so the implementation cannot silently choose otherwise, and
so the next session does not re-derive what was already refuted here.*

---

## 0. What MTW is for, in one paragraph

A fixed-FFT transfer function has one time window for every frequency. Choose it
long and the low end resolves but the high end admits every late reflection as
if it were signal — L2 §1 already recorded that *no estimator fixes that; only
windowing does*. Choose it short and the high end is clean but 4096 points at
48 kHz give 11.7 Hz bins, useless below 200 Hz. MTW gives every frequency region
its own window: long at the bottom, short at the top. Smaart has shipped it as
the default since v7; SIM did it with "a patchwork of 8 or so linear
computations" (McCarthy). This lane builds ours, and the spec's acceptance is
still the one written in `docs/specs/2026-08-26-rta-tool-design.md` §"Where it
must beat": *better than 1/48 octave from 60 Hz up, ~800+ points, compared
against a long fixed FFT in the overlap region*.

## 1. Provenance: what the research found, and what it did not

**Smaart** (Rational Acoustics, v7 guide p.82; v8 guide pp.44, 68, 118; *What's
New in v9* pp.19–22): MTW uses "a series of sample rate decimations and varying
FFT sizes"; the sizes "are not user selectable" and are **not published
anywhere**; ~840 points at 48 kHz; better than 1 Hz at the bottom; longest window
"a little over one second"; Hann. v9 computes **both** MTW and a single FFT on
every engine, and admits two open problems in print: which dataset to display,
and that averaging in seconds is ill-defined because "the actual integration time
varies by frequency". A "Use MTW Coh" switch exists because MTW coherence is the
thing people actually want from it. No patent was found.

**SysTune TFC** (AFMG manual 1.3 p.101) and **Open Sound Meter LTW**
(`src/math/fouriertransform.cpp`, `prepareLog()`) both take the other road: a
*continuous* frequency-dependent window with no band edges. OSM runs 264
log-spaced bands, each a windowed single-bin DFT at full rate with
`N_i * f_i = 1'344'000` constant — about 28 cycles per window at 48 kHz, 24
points per octave. AFMG says in print that band-based schemes have
"discontinuities or gaps". Neither publishes a measured seam error.

**Nobody documents**: the band table, the stitching rule, phase continuity at
the seams, magnitude normalisation across resolutions, where the reference delay
is applied in a multirate chain, or any verification against a reference. That
last gap is the one this project exists to fill, and §7 does.

**This repo** (survey, `file:line` in the station-1 report): `DualFftEngine`
takes equal-length spans of any block size, rings internally, and emits averaged
`Sxx/Syy/Sxy` for one `fftSize`/`hopSize` (`DualFftEngine.h:34-56, :72`).
Reference delay is an integer-sample stream skip *before* the transform
(`:50`, `DualFftEngine.cpp:116-127`). Exponential averaging is specified in
seconds and converted through the hop (`DualFftEngine.cpp:66-67`).
`TransferSnapshot` carries **one scalar `binWidthHz`** (`TransferEstimator.h:60-71`);
`measure::Snapshot` carries **one `fftSize`** and no frequency vector
(`app/src/measure/Snapshot.h:68-101`); `Trace` derives its axis from `fftSize`
and refuses anything else (`app/src/trace/Trace.h:44-61, :91-93`). The IEC
filter bank **removed** its decimation cascade on 2026-08-27 with the rule
"decimation returns only with a profiler result in hand"
(`docs/dsp/2026-08-27-filterbank.md:6-15`). `DualFftEngine.cpp` is at 321 lines
and `test_dualfft.cpp` at exactly 400: neither may grow.

## 2. Decision: no decimation. One `DualFftEngine` per octave, at full rate, FFT size doubling downward

**Decision.** The MTW engine owns `K+1` instances of the existing
`DualFftEngine`, all fed the same full-rate reference/measurement blocks. Band
`k` (k = 0 is the top) has `fftSize N_k = N_0 * 2^k` and `hopSize N_k / 4`.
Band `k` **owns** the octave `[fs / (8 * 2^k), fs / (4 * 2^k))`; the top band
owns up to Nyquist, the bottom band down to DC. The stitched output is the
concatenation of each band's owned bins. Defaults: `N_0 = 1024`, `K = 6`,
window Hann (the fixed engine's default and Smaart's), reference delay applied
at full rate in integer samples. Implementation choice (station 3): every band
engine carries the same `referenceDelaySamples` itself rather than a shared
upstream skip — the skip logic then stays inside `DualFftEngine`, which may not
grow, and each engine re-arms its own skip on `reset()`; the cost is repeating
a one-time skip of `D` samples `K+1` times. Because every band runs at full
rate, an integer `D` is exact in every band, which a decimated design could not
say.

**Why this is the same measurement Smaart makes.** Decimating by `2^k` and
taking a 1024-point FFT covers `1024 * 2^k / fs` seconds; so does a
`1024 * 2^k`-point FFT at full rate. Same window duration, same bin width, same
Hann leakage per bin, same PSD (PSD scaling `2/(fs * sum w^2)` is rate-invariant
by construction, `PsdScaling.h:22-25`). In the owned octave the two are
identical up to the anti-alias filter, and for a transfer function that filter
cancels: if both channels pass the same `G`, then `Sxy' = |G|^2 Sxy` and
`Sxx' = |G|^2 Sxx`, so `H1' = H1` and `gamma^2' = gamma^2` exactly wherever
`|G| != 0`. Decimation is an implementation economy from 2003, not a measurement
property. Note what does **not** cancel: aliasing. A real anti-alias filter
folds energy from `f_alias` into bin `f`, and since `H(f) != H(f_alias)` in any
real system that fold is an additive mix of two transfer values, not a common
factor. The full-rate design has no such path at all; a decimated one is only
as good as its stopband, per stage.

**The argument for decimation, which must be recorded because the roadmap
assumed it.** The master plan's L3 row says "decimation cascade, per-band FFT
sizes, stitching", and Smaart's own text says decimation. The cost it saves is
CPU and memory on the low bands. Measured against this machine's budget, with
a real-FFT cost of `2.5 * N * log2 N` flops: band `k` runs one `N_k`-point FFT
per channel every `N_k/4` samples, i.e. `10 * log2(N_k)` flops per input sample
per channel per band — 160 for the 65536-point band — so all seven bands
together cost **910 flops per sample, about 44 MFLOP/s per channel** at 48 kHz
(the first draft of this record said 400 and 20; the verifier recomputed it).
The filterbank record measured a 43 M multiply-add/s bank as "irrelevant on the
analysis thread of a desktop CPU" and made the rule; this is the same number.

Memory is **not** small and the first draft got it wrong by an order of
magnitude. `DualFftEngine` rings hold `bit_ceil(4 * fftSize + skip)` floats per
channel (`DualFftEngine.cpp:23-25`), so the 65536 band alone carries 2 MB of
rings and the seven bands about 4 MB. The FIFO storage is allocated
**unconditionally**, whatever the averaging mode (`DualFftEngine.cpp:76-78`), at
`fifoDepth * numBins * 32 bytes` per band; with one depth for every band (§5)
and `sum_k numBins_k = 65031` for K = 6 that is **2.08 MB per frame of depth**:
33 MB at the default depth 16, 67 MB at the cap of 32. Two rules follow: in
Exponential mode `MtwEngine` passes `fifoDepth = 1` to every band so the dead
weight is 2 MB, not 33; and `validate()` rejects `fifoDepth > 32`.
This is the one real advantage a decimated design keeps — its bands all stay at
N = 1024 — and 6–70 MB on a desktop analysis thread does not buy back the
filter design, aliasing and fractional-delay work listed below.
Decimation would add: a half-band anti-alias filter to design, prove and golden
(`filter_design_has_no_polynomial_form` forbids `ba`-form design and
`signal.lfilter`, so even the Python side would need care), aliasing margins to
measure per stage, fractional reference delays at decimated rates (the survey
confirms the engine has **no** fractional path and `DelayFinder::subSample` is
already a documented gap), and a cascade whose transient group delay shifts the
bands in time by up to ~40 ms relative to each other. All of that buys 20
MFLOP/s. **Rejected on the record's own rule; the profiler result that would
reopen it does not exist.**

**The argument for OSM's continuous window, which is the stronger opponent.**
LTW has no seams, one update rate for every band, and log-spaced output by
construction. Its costs for *this* project: a second cross-spectrum,
averaging, coherence and gate implementation to prove (nothing in L2 is reused —
OSM's is a bank of single-bin sliding DFTs, not FFTs); ~3 M complex MACs per
channel per update at 264 bands (OSM survives it only with hand-packed SSE);
28 cycles per window, i.e. 24 points per octave — **fails the spec's 48-PPO
target outright**, and raising it to 128 cycles multiplies the cost by four; and
a `Norm`/`Align` normalisation per band that would be a fourth scaling
convention beside the two L2 §5 already refused to add to. The *no seams*
property is real and is addressed in §4: the seam is physics, and hiding it is
the wrong answer for a measurement tool.

**The argument for FTW/TFC-style post-windowing of the IR** (Smaart's FTW
smoothing, AFMG's TFC): cheap, applied on top of a long fixed FFT, no new engine.
It does not give the high bands a faster update or their own coherence, which is
what "Use MTW Coh" and the v9 dual-engine design exist for. Worth a gap entry
of its own; not MTW.

## 3. The band table, and the numbers it produces at 48 kHz

| k | `N_k` | delta-f (Hz) | window | owned range (Hz) | points | cycles in window |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1024 | 46.875 | 21.3 ms | 6000 – 24000 (Nyquist) | 385 | 128 – 512 |
| 1 | 2048 | 23.44 | 42.7 ms | 3000 – 6000 | 128 | 128 – 256 |
| 2 | 4096 | 11.72 | 85.3 ms | 1500 – 3000 | 128 | 128 – 256 |
| 3 | 8192 | 5.859 | 171 ms | 750 – 1500 | 128 | 128 – 256 |
| 4 | 16384 | 2.930 | 341 ms | 375 – 750 | 128 | 128 – 256 |
| 5 | 32768 | 1.465 | 683 ms | 187.5 – 375 | 128 | 128 – 256 |
| 6 | 65536 | 0.7324 | 1.365 s | 0 – 187.5 | 256 | 0 – 256 |

Total **1281 points** (385 + 5 * 128 + 256). Because `delta-f_k = fs / N_k` and
the owned range is `[fs / (8 * 2^k), fs / (4 * 2^k))`, the owned bins of every
band are the **same indices, `N_0/8 .. N_0/4 - 1 = 128 .. 255`**, whatever `k`
(the first draft wrote `N_k` here, which is wrong by a factor `2^k`). The top
band owns bins 128..512 inclusive of Nyquist; the bottom band owns 0..255. Each
boundary frequency `fs / (8 * 2^k)` is bin 128 of band `k` and would be bin 256
of band `k+1`, which band `k+1` does **not** own — so the vector is strictly
increasing with no gap and no duplicate at any seam, including 187.5 Hz. Every
middle band is 128 bins wide and the local resolution runs from **89 to 177
points per octave** (`128 * ln 2` at the bottom of each octave). Below
93.75 Hz the bottom band's fixed 0.73 Hz bins thin out: 57 PPO at 60 Hz,
38 PPO at 40 Hz. The spec's "better than 1/48 octave from 60 Hz up" is met with
`K = 6`; **"beat it at the bottom octave" is `K = 7`** (0.366 Hz, 76 PPO at
40 Hz) at the price of a 2.73 s window, i.e. 2.73 s before the bottom band
shows anything and a 2.73 s memory of whatever the operator just changed.
`K` is therefore a configuration value with default 6, not a constant.

Every band sees **128–256 cycles** of its owned frequencies. That is a
constant-Q design with Q between 128 and 256, and it is where this engine sits
between its neighbours: OSM's 28 cycles trade resolution for reverberation
rejection; SysTune's user-set 10 cycles at 1 kHz more so. `N_0` is the single
knob that moves the whole table (512 halves every window and every PPO figure),
so it is a configuration value too, restricted to `{512, 1024, 2048}`. The
default 1024 is forced by the 48-PPO target: `N_0/8 * ln 2 >= 48  =>  N_0 >= 554`.

## 4. Stitching: hard cut at the owned-octave edge, seams exported, no cross-fade

**Decision.** Each output point comes from exactly one band. The output carries
an explicit frequency vector and a per-band descriptor (`firstIndex`, `fftSize`,
`windowSeconds`, `effectiveAverages`) so a view can draw the seams. There is
**no** blending of adjacent bands.

**Why the seam is real and must stay visible.** For an LTI system measured with
output noise, every band's `H1` is an unbiased estimate of the same `H`; the
only discontinuity at a seam is variance plus resolution, and §7's closed-form
tests pin it at zero for a smooth `H`. In a room it is different, and the
difference is the point of MTW. For a reflection at lag `tau` with gain `a`,
white excitation and window `w` of length `N`, the frame cross-spectrum is

```
E[Sxy]  ~  1 + a * r_w(tau) * exp(-j w tau),
r_w(tau) = sum_n w[n] * w[n + tau]  /  sum_n w[n]^2
```

so a band **admits a reflection in proportion to its window's autocorrelation
at that lag** — the same `overlapCorrelation()` L2 already uses for `Neff`
(`AverageCount.h:20-21`). `r_w` is 1 at lag 0 and exactly 0 for `tau >= N`. A
30 ms reflection is fully in the 1.365 s bottom band's `|H|` as a comb, and
absent from the 21 ms top band's `|H|` — where it lowers **coherence** instead,
to `1/(1 + a^2)` (§7, T4). At the 187.5 Hz seam the two neighbours therefore
legitimately disagree by the comb depth. Cross-fading would draw a smooth curve
through a step that the room actually has, which is exactly the "under-resolved
bands with no qualification" habit the design spec refuses. Smaart lives with
the step; AFMG smooths it and documents a level change as the price. We show
it and mark it.

**Cost of the rejected cross-fade, recorded.** It would need both bands to
compute an overlap region (they already do), an interpolation onto the finer
grid, and a weight law nobody has published; and it would make the golden
vector depend on that law. Deferred, not forbidden: if a probe on measured rooms
ever shows seam steps that mislead operators, the probe and its numbers go here
first.

## 5. Averaging is specified in frames, uniform across bands; seconds are reported per band

**Decision.** `MtwEngine::Config` carries the fixed engine's own averaging
vocabulary and nothing new: `TransferAveraging {Fifo, Exponential}`,
`fifoDepth` in **frames**, `timeConstantFrames` in **frames**. Every band gets
the same depth, hence the same `alpha = 1 - exp(-1 / timeConstantFrames)` and
the same asymptotic `Neff`. Each band reports its own `integrationSeconds =
depth * hop_k / fs` in the band descriptor, and the view shows it. Defaults:
`Fifo`, depth 16 (the fixed engine's default), `fifoDepth` capped at 32 in
`validate()`; in `Exponential` mode every band engine is constructed with
`fifoDepth = 1` because `DualFftEngine` allocates its FIFO unconditionally.

**Why, and why the first draft said the opposite.** The first draft specified
seconds, uniform across bands, on the argument that an operator turning a knob
means seconds. The station-3 planner then computed what the L2 §3 coherence
gate (`minimumEffectiveAverages = 8`) does to that choice, using the real
`exponentialEffectiveAverages`, which is `1 + (Neff_raw - 1) / D` with
`D = 1 + 2 * sum_m c(m * hop)^2 = 1.9246` for Hann at 75 % overlap (identical
in every band, since `hop / N = 1/4` everywhere):

| tau (s), uniform | Neff at 1024 | Neff at 16384 | Neff at 32768 | Neff at 65536 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 (inherited) | 97.9 | 6.58 | 3.55 | **2.06** |
| 2.0 | 390 | 24.8 | 12.7 | **6.58** |
| 3.5 | 682 | 43.1 | 21.8 | 11.1 |

At the inherited 0.5 s **three bands never reach the gate**; the coherence §6
makes the display default would never appear below 750 Hz. The smallest
half-second that clears it with margin is 3.5 s, at which the top band is
averaging 682 independent looks — sixty times what the gate asks — and responds
to an EQ move with a 3.5 s lag at exactly the frequencies where an operator
makes EQ moves. FIFO in seconds is worse: no depth under a sane memory cap
opens the bottom band (6.5 at 4 s; 6.5 s would be needed). The physics is
simple: `Neff` per second of integration scales as `1 / T_window`, so uniform
seconds *cannot* give uniform confidence. What the gate, the coherence value and
the variance of `|H|` all measure is `Neff`; uniform `Neff` is uniform
**frames**. That is what Smaart v9 ships (a bare 1–10 depth), and the thing it
got wrong is only the label — it stopped telling the operator the seconds.
We keep the frames and print the seconds per band.

**What it costs, in the numbers the operator will see** (defaults, 48 kHz):
the gate opens at frame 15 in every band (`fifoEffectiveAverages` = 8.07 at
15 frames, saturating at **8.587** at depth 16; the planner computed both from
`AverageCount.cpp`), i.e. after about `15 * hop_k / fs`: 80 ms at the top
band, 0.64 s at 750–1500 Hz, 2.6 s at 187–375 Hz, **5.1 s below 187.5 Hz**.
The default clears the gate but does not clear it by 25 %; that is the
fixed engine's existing default and MTW inherits it rather than inventing a
second one. K = 7 doubles the bottom figure to 10.9 s, which is one more reason
it is not the default. The view must show the per-band seconds, because a
coherence trace that fills in from the top over five seconds is otherwise read
as a fault.

**Cost of the rejected alternatives.** *Uniform seconds*: a 3.5 s default, or
LF coherence that never appears (table above). *A per-band tau in seconds*:
seven knobs pretending to be one. *Lowering the gate for MTW*: publishes the
number the L2 §3 gate exists to suppress; refused. *Reporting nothing about
seconds*: Smaart's mistake; refused.

**The gate stays where it is and is applied per band**, by routing each band
through the existing `makeSnapshot()`.

**The guard is a tripwire, not a type check, and the design must respect
that.** `check_coherence_gate.cmake` greps every `core/` source except
`TransferEstimator.cpp` for the literal tokens `.coherence =`,
`->coherence =`, `coherence = std::vector`, `coherence.emplace`
(`check_coherence_gate.cmake:27, 44-47`). A stitched struct in `core/` that
*copied* per-band coherence into its own `coherence` field would trip it, and
one that copied into a field of any other name would evade it. Therefore the
core-side result **does not copy coherence at all**: `MtwResult` holds the
per-band `TransferSnapshot`s as produced by `makeSnapshot()` plus the frequency
vector and a `(band, bin)` index per stitched point, and readers go through the
index. The copy into a flat per-point array happens once, in `app/`
(`Analyser.cpp` already does exactly this for the fixed engine, `:172-180`),
where the guard does not reach and the gate has already been applied. This
keeps the guard's one exception file the only place a coherence value is ever
assigned in `core/`.

## 6. Concurrency (G2) and what the app must grow

**Decision.** `MtwEngine` lives in `core/` and, like `DualFftEngine`, takes
spans and returns numbers. In `app/`, `Analyser` hosts **both** engines and
feeds each the same reference/measurement pair per hop (`Analyser.h:125-131`
already owns one of each engine type, so this is a second member, not a second
thread). `measure::Snapshot` gains `std::optional<MtwBlock>` with an **explicit
frequency vector**; the fixed `TransferBlock` is untouched. `TransferView` draws
the MTW block through the same `geometry.xForHz` it already uses for stored
traces, taking `hz` from the vector instead of `i * binHz`, and marks the seams.
Display default: **MTW for magnitude, phase and coherence, fixed FFT
selectable per plot** — Smaart's default since v7 and the reason its v9 added
"Use MTW Coh". This is a default the owner may override; it is not a blocking
decision and is not queued.

**Out of this lane, stated so nobody assumes it slipped in:** storing an MTW
trace. `Trace` derives its axis from `fftSize` on purpose ("one source of
truth for the axis", `Trace.h:91-93`); giving it a frequency vector touches
`Trace`, `SessionCodec`, `TraceBlobCodec` and every `columnsForBins` call site.
That is an L5 amendment with its own record, and the MTW block is live-only
until it lands. Also out: MTW for the single-channel RTA (the banding record's
hope at `2026-08-26-banding-and-averaging.md:200-203` — the per-band `Sxx` is
available from the same engines and can be picked up later without redesign),
MTW coherence weighting for L6b, and any cross-fade.

## 7. How CI proves this with no sound card

Expectations come from identities or from a golden, per CLAUDE.md, in this
order. Tolerances on anything FFT-derived use the two-term form in
`memory/float32-fft-precision.md`; the 65536-point band has more rounding
than the 1024-point band and the tolerance must be computed from the band's own
peak, not a global one.

- **T1 — Axis identity.** The stitched frequency vector is strictly increasing;
  point `i` of band `k` equals `bin * fs / N_k`; owned boundaries are exactly
  `fs / (8 * 2^k)`; total count for the defaults is 1281; changing `N_0` or `K`
  moves it by the closed form in §3. Pure functions, no signal.
- **T2 — Compensated pure delay is unity everywhere.** `y = x` delayed by `D`
  samples, `referenceDelaySamples = D`: every band, every owned bin, `H = 1`
  and `gamma^2 = 1` (the frames are identical after the skip, so this is exact
  up to float32). Also with `D` not divisible by any `2^k`, which is the case a
  decimated design could not have passed without a fractional delay.
- **T3 — A known LTI system reads back its closed form across every seam.** A
  biquad with analytic `H(e^{jw})` driven by white noise, long averaging: the
  stitched `|H|` and phase match the closed form at every point within a
  tolerance derived from `Neff` and the Hann leakage of a smooth response.
  Any seam step on a smooth system fails this test; there is no separate seam
  test to weaken.
- **T4 — Reflection admission equals the window autocorrelation.**
  `y = x + a * x(t - tau)`, white `x`. For each band, expected
  `H = 1 + a * r_w(tau) * exp(-j w tau)` with `r_w` from `overlapCorrelation()`,
  and `gamma^2 = |1 + a r_w e^{-jw tau}|^2 / (1 + a^2 + 2 a r_w cos(w tau))`.
  With `tau = 30 ms`, `a = 0.5`: the top band reads `|H| = 1`, `gamma^2 = 0.8`;
  the bottom band reads the comb and `gamma^2 ~ 1`. This is the test that
  proves MTW does the thing it is for, and it also pins the sign of the seam
  step.
- **T5 — Frames are frames, seconds are reported.** For the default config every
  band's `alpha` is identical and equals `1 - exp(-1/16)`; every band's
  `fifoDepth` is 16; after `M` frames in every band, `effectiveAverages_k` is
  identical across bands (via the real `fifoEffectiveAverages`), first clears
  the gate at frame 15 (8.07) and saturates at 8.587 at depth 16; `integrationSeconds_k = 16 * hop_k / fs` equals the §5
  figures (0.085, 0.171, 0.341, 0.683, 1.365, 2.731, 5.461 s). `validate()`
  rejects depth 33 and accepts 32. The negative half: a uniform-seconds
  configuration at 0.5 s leaves the 65536 band under the gate asymptotically —
  asserted so nobody re-introduces seconds without meeting the table in §5.
- **T6 — Golden.** `tools/gen_mtw.py` extends `gen_transfer.py`'s pattern:
  float32 noise pair through a known system, `scipy.signal.csd` per band with
  `nperseg = N_k`, `noverlap = 3 N_k / 4`, Hann, `average="mean"`, sliced to the
  owned bins and concatenated; committed under `core/tests/golden/`. The
  generator must take an argument (memory: *a gen script runs the moment you
  invoke it*), must not call `signal.lfilter` (guard), and runs from the main
  checkout's venv, not a worktree.
- **T7 — Guards.** `core_has_no_framework_deps` file count rises by the new
  `core/` files and is made RED once by an inserted include, then GREEN (the
  L4a/L4b procedure). `coherence_gate_is_not_bypassed` stays the only place
  coherence is assigned. No file over 400 lines; `DualFftEngine.cpp` and
  `test_dualfft.cpp` are **not edited** (321 and 400 lines).

The spec's own acceptance — "compare MTW output against a long fixed FFT in the
overlap region" — is subsumed: the bottom band *is* a 65536-point fixed engine,
and T3 checks every other band against the closed form the long FFT would also
have to match.

## 8. What this record does not decide

- Cross-fading at seams (§4: rejected pending a probe on measured rooms).
- Storing MTW traces, trace maths on them, session persistence (L5 amendment).
- FTW/TFC-style post-windowing of the impulse response (new gap entry; not MTW).
- MTW-driven RTA banding and MTW coherence weighting for spatial averaging (L6b).
- Whether `K = 7` becomes the default once the 2.73 s fill is seen on a real
  system. Measure on stage first.
- The G21/G25 polarity and gating tools do not depend on MTW (parity record).
