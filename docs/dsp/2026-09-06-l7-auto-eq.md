## 0. What L7-EQ is for, and the owner's two rulings

An operator has a measured transfer function `H` with its coherence (L2), a
target (L5b's corridor, or any trace), and a parametric EQ somewhere downstream
— the tool's own virtual EQ (G11) or a desk. L7-EQ turns the deviation between
the two into a small set of biquad filters, shows the predicted result before
anything is committed, and **refuses to boost where boosting cannot work**.
The last clause is the whole difficulty: REW and AutoEq solve "fit this curve";
a live PA needs "fit this curve, except where the dip is not a filter's to fix".

Two owner rulings shape the record. (1) The generator output path is L7's
shared prerequisite (`docs/HUMAN-QA-QUEUE.md` "Từ lane L6b", 2026-09-06); its
interface is `docs/dsp/2026-09-06-l7-output-path.md` §6 and is **cited, not
redesigned** (§8). (2) **G24 — the minimum/excess-phase decomposition,
scoped to L4c in `docs/plans/MASTER-EXECUTION-PLAN.md:120` and to P4 in the
parity table row `G24` — is folded into this sub-lane** (owner, 2026-09-06,
relayed in the orchestrator's brief; not yet written into the plan or the
queue — §12 asks for that). This record therefore designs the decomposition
**as far as EQ needs it**: a per-dip test that says *boostable* or *not*. The
L4c display feature reuses the same kernel (§4.5) and is not designed here.

Four premises are corrected here so nobody builds on them:

1. **"The coherence gate is already built" is half true.** What ships is L2's
   `nullopt` floor (`minimumEffectiveAverages = 8`, guarded) and L6b's weight
   `W = u·γ²`. The *thresholded* gate V1 §3 describes belongs to L5b, which has
   **no record yet**; the only threshold in `app/` is a mockup constant
   (`dev/preview/TransferFunctionPreview.cpp:78`). The allocator therefore
   takes a caller-supplied `trusted` mask (§4.1) and owns no threshold.
2. **"Reflection nulls are non-minimum-phase" is false in general, and the
   truth is the reason this record's test exists.** A direct path plus one
   reflection is `H = 1 + a·e^{−jωτ}`. For `a < 1` every zero of
   `1 + a·z^{−D}` lies at radius `a^{1/D} < 1`: the comb is **minimum-phase**
   and its notches are, in principle, invertible. For `a > 1` the zeros lie
   outside and the notches are not. The two cases have **identical magnitude
   shapes** — `|1 + a·e^{−jθ}| = a·|1 + (1/a)·e^{−jθ}|` exactly — so no
   magnitude-only allocator, however capped, can tell them apart (§4.2).
3. **The minimum-phase machinery was being specified twice.** The FIR-export
   record (`2026-09-06-l7-fir-export.md` §4) already commits `core/` to the
   homomorphic real-cepstrum method with the −120 dB floor. This record does
   not specify a second one: §4.3 names one shared kernel.
4. **"The allocator runs on the analysis thread"** overstates it. It runs on
   an operator action, over an immutable snapshot, on the message thread or
   a worker; never in the audio callback and never per block. Its cost is
   bounded in §7.

## 2. Decision: two modes, one algorithm, two stopping points

**Decision.** The names are **Auto EQ** and **Suggest**, fixed by the spec and
refined here. *Auto EQ*: one action runs the allocator (§3) to `N` filters
(default 6, capped by a UI control), solves all gains jointly, and lands the
whole set on the virtual EQ with the predicted post-EQ trace drawn as a ghost;
the operator accepts, trims, or discards. *Suggest*: the same allocator runs
**one greedy step** and returns the top 1–3 ranked candidates as chips; the
operator accepts one, the residual is recomputed, and the ranking is redone.
There is one implementation with a stopping parameter, not two solvers.

**Two things the spec left open, decided.** *Declining a chip* adds that
candidate's region to a session exclusion mask the allocator already consumes
for gates (§4); the next ranking omits it. *What "the residual" is after an
acceptance*: the virtual EQ is a simulation and the measurement does not move
when a chip is accepted, so the residual for re-ranking is
`measured − target + Σ R_i` over committed filters **not yet marked applied**.
A filter the operator has dialled into the real DSP is marked *applied* (the
freeze-ghost of V1 §8 is the detector that proposes the mark) and leaves the
sum, or its correction would be counted twice — once in the room, once in the
prediction. Suggest therefore serves both workflows the research described
without preferring either.

## 3. Decision: hybrid allocator — greedy placement, closed-form weighted ridge least squares for the gains

**Decision.** Placement `(fc, Q, type)` is greedy on the residual; gains are
solved jointly by weighted ridge least squares in dB. Details:

- **Residual** `r_k = m_k − t_k − c`, where `m` is the measured magnitude in dB,
  `t` the target (§5), and `c` the auto-offset: the `γ²/f`-weighted mean of
  `m − t` over the anchor band (V1 §1; a weighted mean is closed form).
- **Placement** reads the residual at V1's judging resolution (1/6 oct by
  default, the operator's own choice), so one noisy bin never places a filter.
  The candidate is the trusted extremum of largest `|r|`; its region
  `[f_L, f_R]` is bounded by the flanking local extrema of the opposite sense.
  **Q comes from the feature's half-depth width**: `Q = fc/(f_2 − f_1)` where
  `r` crosses half its extremum — and this is the cookbook's own definition
  (the peaking filter's bandwidth is measured between its `G/2` dB points, §6),
  so the filter's half-gain points sit on the feature's half-depth points by
  construction. Clamped to `[Q_min, Q_max]` (§3, caps).
- **Gain solve.** For fixed `(fc_i, Q_i)`, the cascade's dB response is
  **exactly** `Σ_i R_i(f; g_i)` (a log of a product). Each `R_i` is linearised
  as `g_i·s_i(f)`, `s_i(f) = R_i(f; 1 dB)`. That linearisation is **exact at
  five frequencies for every gain** — `fc` (`R = g`), the two midpoint
  frequencies (`R = g/2`, gain-independent, §6), DC and Nyquist (`R = 0`) —
  and approximate between them; `gen_autoeq.py` measures the largest
  `|R_i(f; g) − g·s_i(f)|` over `g ∈ [−15, +6]` dB and the figure is amended
  into this file. Then `g = argmin Σ_k w_k (r_k − Σ_i s_i(f_k) g_i)² + λ‖g‖²`,
  solved as `(SᵀWS + λI) g = SᵀW r` by Cholesky in double: `N ≤ 16` unknowns,
  one solve, no iteration, no tolerance, no initial guess.
- **Weights** `w_k = γ²_k / f_k` on trusted bins, zero elsewhere. `γ²` is
  L6b §3's bounded trust (the inverse-variance weight was rejected there for
  99:1 dominance and the argument transfers: the residual here is dominated
  by model misfit — six filters cannot fit the curve — not by estimation
  noise, so Gauss–Markov's premise fails). `1/f` is the Jacobian of the log
  axis: a linear bin grid puts most of its bins above 2 kHz, and without it
  the fit serves the top octave at the expense of the bottom three.
- **Ridge `λ`, derived not chosen.** Its job is to keep the solve finite when
  two placed filters overlap (near-collinear columns). For an *isolated*
  filter ridge shrinks the gain by the factor `sᵀWs/(sᵀWs + λ)`; `λ` is set
  so that shrinkage at the boost cap is **exactly 0.1 dB** — the one-decimal
  dB resolution `CLAUDE.md` calls the smallest actionable difference — using
  the smallest column norm among placed filters, so no filter is shrunk more:
  `λ = 0.1·min_i(s_iᵀWs_i)/(G_cap − 0.1)`. The primary defence against
  collinearity is a **placement rule**: no new filter within half the
  bandwidth of an existing one — it merges into it instead — so ridge is a
  safety net. `cond(SᵀWS + λI)` is carried in the result.
- **Caps.** Per-filter boost `≤ G_cap` (default +6 dB, **a judgement**,
  labelled: a boost of `G` dB costs exactly `G` dB of system headroom at that
  frequency, which is why boosts are capped and cuts are not). `Q_max`: the
  filter's ring time is closed form, `t_60 = (ln 1000/π)·Q_p/fc = 2.2·Q_p/fc`
  with `Q_p = A·Q` for a cookbook boost (`A = 10^{G/40}`); **when an L4b
  T60 exists in the session, `Q_max` is the Q at which `t_60` equals the
  room's T60 in that band** — a filter that rings longer than the room is
  heard as a resonance, one that rings shorter is masked by it. Without a
  T60 the fallback is `Q ≤ 10` for boosts and `Q ≤ 20` for cuts (a judgement,
  chosen for export compatibility with common hardware PEQ ranges; §12).
- **Shelves.** A low or high shelf is placed only where the residual keeps
  one sign across the outermost half-octave of the trusted band (REW's edge
  rule, adopted as a placement rule); its gain is then one more column of
  `S`. **No all-pass**: `|H_ap| ≡ 1`, so its column in `S` is identically
  zero — an amplitude objective cannot place one. Phase belongs to V2.

**Against the obvious — global nonlinear optimisation over all `(fc, Q, g)`
(AutoEq's `fmin_slsqp` with a slope penalty).** Three reasons, each with a
cost. (1) It has no closed form: the result depends on the solver, its
tolerance and its start, so proving it means freezing one SciPy version's
output as a golden — the "whatever the code printed" pattern `CLAUDE.md`
forbids — or writing and proving a constrained nonlinear solver in `core/`,
which is a lane, not a feature. (2) It solves the wrong problem: AutoEq's
domain is headphones, near-field, no reflections; its penalty discourages
*ringing*, which is a smoothness problem. This project's hard problem is
*null avoidance*, which no objective over `|H|` can express — §1.2 is the
proof: two inputs with identical `|H|` need opposite decisions. A global
optimiser would confidently boost the `a > 1` comb. (3) It has no first
suggestion: a joint solve yields all `N` or nothing, so Suggest would re-solve
the whole problem per chip and lose the "what did my last move do" model V1's
honesty rules are built on. **What the hybrid costs:** greedy placement is
order-dependent and can need one or two more filters than a joint fit for the
same flatness; the gain linearisation is inexact between its five exact
points (measured, §9); and the joint gain solve does nothing to *move* a
badly placed filter. Those costs are visible in the ghost, which is the exact
cascade response, never the linear model.

**Against REW's pure greedy, per-filter gain.** Each step zeros its own bin
without accounting for the other `N − 1` filters' skirts there; two adjacent
filters then fight, and the operator sees a ghost that misses by the overlap.
The joint gain solve removes that at the price of one `N×N` solve.

## 4. Decision: null-avoidance is two independent gates; the second is designed here

### 4.1 Gate 1 — coherence: trust in the data

A bin enters the residual only if `coherence` is present (L2 §3 floor) **and**
`γ²_k ≥ θ`, with `θ` supplied by the caller as a `trusted` mask — L5b owns the
threshold, as L5c §"not decided" already ruled. This gate is a data-quality
refusal and **cannot** catch a coherent null: a reflection is deterministic and
coherent with the reference, so under the additive-noise model
`γ² = |H|²Sxx/(|H|²Sxx + Snn)` a notch lowers coherence only where the noise
floor is reached. With 40 dB broadband SNR a 12 dB notch reads `γ² = 0.998`,
a 20 dB notch `0.990`, a 30 dB notch `0.909`; only a 40 dB notch falls to
`0.5`. Every gate a human would set passes the first three.

### 4.2 The physics the second gate rests on

Any `H` factors as `H = H_min · H_ap` with `|H_min| = |H|`, `|H_ap| = 1`; the
phase of `H_ap` is the **excess phase**. Every cookbook peaking or shelf
section is minimum-phase (numerator and denominator share one form with `A`
inverted; both root pairs lie inside the circle for `A > 0`), so a biquad EQ
can invert `H_min` and can do **nothing** to `H_ap`. A dip is EQ's to fix iff
it lives in `H_min`, i.e. the excess phase is flat across it. The two-path
identity of §1.2 makes this operational: for `a > 1`,
`H = a(1 + (1/a)z^{−D}) · z^{−D}(1 + (1/a)z^{D})/(1 + (1/a)z^{−D})`, so the
excess phase is a pure delay of `D` samples plus `−2·arg(1 + r·e^{−jωD})`,
`r = 1/a`, whose swing across one notch is **exactly `4·arcsin r`**; for
`a < 1` the excess phase is identically zero. Two hypotheses, two closed-form
values, and only the phase separates them.

### 4.3 The test (G24 as EQ needs it)

1. **Input.** The **fixed-engine** `TransferSnapshot` (linear grid, complex
   `h`, phase already delay-compensated before the FFT per L2 §4). Not the
   stitched MTW trace: a cepstrum is a DFT and needs one uniform grid; the
   fixed engine runs concurrently with MTW by L3's decision.
2. **Fill.** Untrusted bins take the magnitude of the nearest trusted bin (a
   flat hold introduces no zeros). The minimum-phase result is *asserted* only
   for dips whose region and a margin of one region-width on each side are
   trusted — the fill edge leaks cepstrally over a scale comparable to the
   feature it borders (the margin width is a judgement, labelled).
3. **Kernel — one, shared with G10.** `rta::dsp::minimumPhaseFromMagnitude`
   (`core/include/rta/dsp/MinimumPhase.h`): floor `|H|` at
   `kMagnitudeFloorDb = −120`, real cepstrum of `ln|H|` via `Fft`, fold
   `[1, 2, …, 2, 1]`, exponentiate — the FIR record §4 steps 1–3 with the log
   **not** halved. Whichever lane builds first creates it; the other calls it.

   **Amendment, 2026-09-15 — what the oversampling factor is actually
   measuring.** The kernel does no oversampling of its own; the caller
   supplies it, and G24's caller ships `kExcessPhaseOversamplingFactor = 128`
   (`core/include/rta/dsp/ExcessPhase.h`), sixteen times FIR's 8. That is the
   right constant, but the reason it is 128 has been stated imprecisely and
   is corrected here. The Task A sweep applies **two** convergence criteria
   to the unwindowed two-path comb — the reconstructed excess-phase **swing**
   (what `classifyDip` reads) and the min-phase impulse's **tail energy**
   past `nFft/2` (a proxy for cepstral aliasing). They do not converge
   together. Measured over the nine `(a, D)` fixtures, the smallest swept `F`
   at which each criterion alone holds:

   | `a` | `D` | swing only | tail only | both |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 1.25 | 37 | 1 | 4 | 4 |
   | 1.25 | 144 | 4 | 16 | 16 |
   | 1.25 | 511 | 8 | **64** | **64** |
   | 2.0 | 37 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
   | 2.0 | 144 | 1 | 4 | 4 |
   | 2.0 | 511 | 4 | 16 | 16 |
   | 4.0 | 37 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
   | 4.0 | 144 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
   | 4.0 | 511 | 2 | 8 | 8 |
   | **worst case** | | **8** | **64** | **64** |

   So the honest sentence is "**64× is the smallest factor at which the
   min-phase impulse's cepstral tail energy falls under the absolute
   floor**", not "the smallest factor at which the swing converges" — the
   swing has already converged at 8× on the worst fixture. 128× still ships
   unchanged: tail energy *is* aliasing, and aliasing corrupts the kernel
   whether or not this particular swing metric has noticed yet, so the
   conservative factor is the defensible one. The correction matters because
   a later reader who believed the swing drove it could cut the factor
   eight-fold and see no test go red until a real measurement with a sharp
   notch arrived. Numbers re-measured 2026-09-15 through
   `tools/gen_autoeq_algo.py`'s own pure functions (no golden regenerated;
   `gen_autoeq.py --check` byte-identical afterwards).
4. **Excess phase** `φ_x = arg h − arg h_min`, wrapped, then the broadband
   delay removed: `τ_0 = median` of the excess group delay
   (`groupDelaySeconds` on `h/h_min`, whose `|·|² = 1` makes its denominator
   perfectly conditioned) over trusted bins, and `φ_x ← φ_x + ωτ_0`. The
   allpass comb term has zero mean group delay per period, so the median
   recovers the delay and leaves the swing.
5. **Per candidate boost.** Depth `D = min(r(f_L), r(f_R)) − r(f*)` in dB
   (peak-to-notch, lower flank — conservative, since a smaller `D` lowers the
   threshold and refuses more). Swing `S = max φ_x − min φ_x` over
   `[f_L, f_R]` at bin resolution (the allpass phase is smooth; smoothing
   would erase a swing concentrated at the notch).
6. **Verdict.** Under the two-path model the notch depth fixes `r`:
   `r_D = (10^{D/20} − 1)/(10^{D/20} + 1)`; the minimum-phase hypothesis
   predicts `S = 0`, the non-minimum-phase one `S = 4·arcsin r_D`. The
   boundary is the midpoint, `S* = 2·arcsin r_D` — the maximum-margin split
   between two closed-form outcomes, computed from the dip's own depth, not a
   grid floor:

   | D (dB) | 3 | 6 | 10 | 12 | 20 | 30 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | `r_D` | 0.171 | 0.332 | 0.519 | 0.599 | 0.818 | 0.939 |
   | NMP swing `4·arcsin r_D` | 39° | 78° | 125° | 147° | 220° | 280° |
   | threshold `2·arcsin r_D` | 20° | 39° | 62° | 74° | 110° | 140° |

   `S < S*` → **Boostable**; `S ≥ S*` → **NotMinimumPhase** ("phase problem,
   not EQ problem", routed to V2 as the spec's honesty rule requires);
   insufficient trusted neighbourhood → **Untrusted**. The result carries
   `D`, `S`, `S*` and the margin `S/S*`, displayed and never re-gated on — the
   `Polarity.h:103-110` lesson. The model assumption is stated with the
   verdict: a dip that is not two-path is classified by the same rule.
7. **Boosts only.** Cuts are not gated: a wrong cut costs level at one
   frequency in one position; a wrong boost costs headroom everywhere and
   adds ringing. The margin is still reported on cut chips.

### 4.4 Against the alternatives

*A boost cap alone (REW's answer).* Blind by §1.2: a 12 dB `a > 1` notch is
inside any sane cap and still not a filter's to fix. *Attribution by the G11
summation ghost (the spec's wording).* Correct, and needs **two captured
sources**; a floor bounce or a wall reflection from one source has no second
capture. §4.3 works from one measurement; the G11 attribution is a later
refinement for the two-source case. *A fixed 90° threshold.* Misclassifies
every non-minimum-phase dip shallower than about 7 dB (78° at 6 dB) — the
depth-derived boundary is necessary, not decorative. *Hilbert transform of
`ln|H|` directly.* The same mathematics (Bode's gain–phase relation) with no
kernel to share; the cepstrum reuses G10's. *Skipping the test until L4c
builds G24.* Ships an allocator that boosts into the one class of dip the
whole feature exists to avoid; the research called this dependency
load-bearing and the owner's fold ruling agrees.

### 4.5 What the L4c display feature reuses, and what it must still design

L4c's G24 display gets `MinimumPhase.h`, the excess-phase computation of
§4.3.4 and the per-bin `φ_x`/excess-group-delay vectors for a pane. It must
still design what this record does not need: the minimum-phase **impulse
response** and excess-phase IR in the time domain, their windowing against
the L4a `Deconvolution`, and how they sit beside the draggable gate (G25).
Those are display and IR questions; the EQ test never needs a time-domain
minimum-phase signal.

## 5. Decision: target types now, and the ISO 2969 dependency stated narrowly

**Ship now, no purchase:** *Flat* with auto-offset (§3). *Tilt*: `t(f) =
k·log2(f/f_pivot)` dB, one slope and one pivot, closed form. *User-drawn*:
`(Hz, dB)` breakpoints interpolated linearly in log-frequency and linearly in
dB — the FIR record §6 rule, shared — including **import from a two-column
text file**, which is the escape hatch that keeps named curves out of the
codebase while letting an operator use any curve they own. *Reference trace*:
a stored L5a trace's magnitude resampled onto the residual grid. The allocator
consumes a target as a dB vector on its grid and never learns its provenance.

**Blocked, and labelled so in the UI:** a preset **named** "X-curve" claiming
ISO 2969 / SMPTE ST 202 conformance — the curve's shape is public, the
**tolerance table** is the purchased part, and a preset carrying the name
without the table is a false conformance claim (the L5b block note). Likewise
a preset **named** "Harman": the numbers live behind an AES paper and nobody
here has verified them against the source (`the-parity-table-is-a-hypothesis`).
The dependency is exactly that narrow: two *names*, not any capability. An
operator who draws the X-curve shape by hand or imports a Harman-like file is
using the user-drawn target and the tool says so.

## 6. Decision: the new biquad-design core work — sized, with its closed-form identities

**Decision.** `rta::eq::designBiquad(FilterSpec, sampleRate) → Biquad::Coeffs`
implements the RBJ Audio EQ Cookbook peaking, low-shelf and high-shelf
sections with the `Q` parameterisation: `A = 10^{G/40}`, `ω_0 = 2π fc/fs`,
`α = sin ω_0/(2Q)`; peaking `b = {1 + αA, −2cos ω_0, 1 − αA}`,
`a = {1 + α/A, −2cos ω_0, 1 − α/A}`, normalised by `a_0`; shelves per the
cookbook's `√A` forms. **The plan re-reads the cookbook text for the shelf
coefficients rather than trusting this transcription** — the identities below
are what the tests assert, and a transcription error fails them.

**Why the identities are exact, not asserted from output.** The cookbook
peaking section *is* the bilinear transform of the analogue prototype
`(s² + s·A/Q + 1)/(s² + s/(AQ) + 1)` prewarped at `ω_0`: with
`K = cot(ω_0/2)`, `(K²−1)/(K²+1) = cos ω_0` and `K/(K²+1) = sin ω_0/2`, which
reproduces `α`. Hence the digital response at `ω` equals the prototype's at
`w = tan(ω/2)/tan(ω_0/2)`, and:

- `|H| = 1` at DC and Nyquist (`Σb = Σa` at `z = ±1`): **0 dB exactly**.
- `|H(e^{jω_0})| = A²`: **`G` dB at `fc` exactly**.
- At `|H| = A` (**`G/2` dB**), `(1−w²)² = w²/Q²`, whose positive roots satisfy
  `w_2 − w_1 = 1/Q` and `w_1 w_2 = 1` **independently of `A`** — the
  cookbook's midpoint-gain bandwidth definition, at digital frequencies
  `ω_{1,2} = 2·arctan(w_{1,2}·tan(ω_0/2))`, `w_{1,2} = ∓1/(2Q) + √(1 + 1/(4Q²))`.
  This is the fact §3's Q-from-half-depth and the five exact linearisation
  points rest on.
- Inverse: `coeffs(G)` cascaded with `coeffs(−G)` has `attenuationDb ≡ 0`.
- Shelves: `G` dB at the shelved end, 0 dB at the other, **`G/2` dB at `fc`**
  (the cookbook shelf's `|H(jω_0)| = A` by construction).
- `maxPoleRadius < 1` for every `G ∈ [−30, +30]`, `Q ∈ [0.1, 20]`,
  `fc ∈ (0, fs/2)` — the `BiquadCascade` invariant the filterbank asserts.

**The polynomial-form guard.** `check_no_polynomial_form.cmake` forbids
high-order `ba` polynomials because their roots leave the unit circle in
double (`filterbank.md` §"representation rule"). A single second-order section
**is** the SOS form that rule mandates; `Biquad::Coeffs` holds nothing else.
`gen_autoeq.py` must still avoid the guard's string patterns: `sosfreqz(sos`
not `freqz(b`, `sosfilt` not `signal.lfilter`, and no array named `b`.

**Size (estimate, not a claim):** `BiquadDesign.{h,cpp}` ≈ 150 lines;
`MinimumPhase.{h,cpp}` ≈ 120 (shared with G10); `ExcessPhase.{h,cpp}` ≈ 150;
`DipClassifier.{h,cpp}` ≈ 120; `EqAllocator.{h,cpp}` ≈ 300 (placement, weights,
ridge solve, ranking) — the one file near the 300 aim, split at the
placement/solve seam if it passes it. Five test files, one generator script.

## 7. Decision: the core/app boundary and the one descriptor three lanes share

**`core/` (`rta::eq`, `core/include/rta/eq/`):** `FilterSpec {type, fcHz, q,
gainDb}`; `designBiquad`; `responseDb(FilterSpec, fs, hz)` (exact, via
`attenuationDb` with the sign flipped); `excessPhase(const TransferSnapshot&,
trusted) → {φ_x, τ_x, τ_0, valid}`; `classifyDip(...) → {verdict, D, S, S*}`;
`rankCandidates(input, maxCandidates)` (one greedy step — Suggest);
`solveGains(placed, input) → {gains, cond}`; `autoEq(input, maxFilters)`
(greedy to `N`, then one solve). `input` is spans: `hz`, `residualDb`,
`coherence`, `trusted`, `excluded`, plus limits. Spans in, numbers out;
`core_has_no_framework_deps` untouched. **Cost, bounded:** per greedy step
one pass over `M ≈ 8193` bins; per candidate one `attenuationDb` per bin
(~20 flops); the cepstrum is two `Fft`s of `fftSize`; the solve is `N ≤ 16`.
Milliseconds on an operator action, on the message thread or a worker, over
a snapshot obtained by the existing atomic swap. **Never in the callback.**

**`app/`:** chips, ranking display, accept/decline/re-rank, the `N` cap, the
*applied* mark (§2), writing accepted `FilterSpec`s into the **virtual EQ**
model — which **does not exist yet**: `app/` holds only preview mockups
(`dev/preview/TargetMatchPreview.cpp`), so the model is L7-G11's to own; this
record defines only what lands in it and the ghost identity
`ghost_k = m_k + Σ_i R_i(f_k)` (exact: dB add). Export of the `FilterSpec`
list as text is `app/` (trivial); FIR export is G10.

**Amendment, 2026-09-15 — the ghost identity carries an *applied* membership
rule, and the text export carries the bit.** Two corrections to the paragraph
above, both found by independent verify of the L7-EQ app build.

1. **`ghost_k = m_k + Σ_i R_i(f_k)` is over the filters NOT marked *applied*,
   not over every committed filter.** §2 already says the *residual* drops an
   applied filter's term ("or its correction would be counted twice — once in
   the room, once in the prediction"); the same sentence settles the ghost,
   because both read the same measurement. The rule stated once, for both:
   `measuredDb` is always the latest measurement and *applied* asserts the
   filter is already in the signal path it came through. `ghost − target ≡
   residual` then holds identically, at any applied state, and that identity
   is what the app-side test asserts. Shipping the two sums with opposite
   membership made the session re-suggest the filter the operator had just
   dialled in — a −7.1 dB cut proposed a second time on an 8 dB bump.
2. **The text export writes a fifth, optional column, `applied`** — a
   deviation from this record's "trivial" and from the impl plan's Task E row,
   which specifies `type, fc, Q, gain` only. A list that drops the bit is
   unsafe to load back into the very processor that produced the measurement:
   the applied rows land twice. The column is written only for applied rows
   and is optional on read, so a hand-written or older four-column file still
   imports, and an unflagged row reads as *not* applied — the safe direction,
   since it surfaces as a filter the operator can see rather than silently
   dropping a correction they asked for.
3. **The parser refuses rather than guesses, and strips the Windows BOM.**
   Two corrections found by the third verify round, both about a file the tool
   did not write. An unrecognised type word is **rejected with its line
   number**, never reshaped into a `Peaking`: the old fallback turned an 80 Hz
   `lowshelf +2 dB` into an 80 Hz *peaking* `+2 dB` — a different filter,
   landed on the rig, that nobody was told about — whenever a UTF-8 byte-order
   mark was glued to the first row's type word, which is what Notepad and
   PowerShell 5.1's `Out-File` / `Set-Content` write by default. A leading BOM
   is therefore stripped before parsing, and `parseFilterList` returns the
   rejected line numbers alongside the rows that imported. CRLF needs no
   handling: `'\r'` is whitespace to the extractor.

**The descriptor shared with G10 and G11.** `FilterSpec` is the type the
virtual processor simulates and the FIR exporter consumes. The FIR record §11
asked whether auto-EQ hands over breakpoints or a per-bin curve: **a per-bin
curve** — `FirDesign` gains an overload taking the target already sampled on
its own design grid, and auto-EQ fills it with `Σ R_i` evaluated exactly there.
Breakpoints would interpolate a high-Q peak by dB-level errors. This amends
FIR §6/§11 by one overload, nothing else.

## 8. VERIFY mode rides the output path as a consumer, and the honesty tolerance is the estimate's own

After the operator applies filters (externally, or marks them applied), the
tool re-measures and compares. Excitation follows `l7-output-path.md` §6
verbatim: `setSource(PinkNoise)` → `routeOutput(ch, true)` → `armSource()` →
wait for the coherence gate (§11: the first 10 ms are not stationary, and the
gate is the wait already in place) → snapshot → `disarmSource()` → wait
`sourceIsQuiescent()`. Nothing in this record touches a gate or the slot.

**"Disagrees beyond tolerance"** (V1 honesty rule 2) is flagged per bin where
`|measured − predicted|` exceeds **both** the operator's corridor half-width
(the tolerance they already set) **and** `3σ_k`, with `σ_k` the estimate's own
standard deviation in dB from Bendat & Piersol's H1 variance,
`σ_k ≈ (20/ln 10)·√((1 − γ²_k)/(2·n_d·γ²_k))` — the form L6b §1 carried with
its equation number unverified, and the same caveat holds. The first term is
what the operator cares about; the second is what makes it not noise. The
view says so and never re-solves silently.

## 9. How CI proves this with no sound card

Core tests in `core/tests/test_biquad_design.cpp`, `test_minimum_phase.cpp`,
`test_excess_phase.cpp`, `test_eq_allocator.cpp`; golden
`core/tests/golden/autoeq.txt` from `tools/gen_autoeq.py` (argparse, `--out`,
`--check`, `--help` exits before any write — the `gen_mtw.py` precedent,
per `a-gen-script-runs-the-moment-you-invoke-it`). Tolerance shapes follow
the FIR record §7: **Shape A** for anything through `Fft`
(`tol_k = 1e-6·|X_k| + 2e-7·peak`, residual printed beside the tolerance and
tightened if an order of magnitude under it — `a-fixture-can-be-too-well-
behaved-to-fail`); **Shape B** for the double-precision solve
(`|g_core − g_golden| ≤ cond·1e-15·max|g_golden| + 1e-12`, `cond` written into
the golden); plain `1e-12` relative for coefficient algebra in double.

1. **Cookbook identities (tier 1).** For a grid of `(fc, Q, G)` at 44.1/48/96
   kHz: 0 dB at DC and Nyquist, `G` at `fc`, `G/2` at `ω_{1,2}` of §6,
   `coeffs(G)∘coeffs(−G)` flat, shelf `G`/0/`G/2`, `maxPoleRadius < 1`.
2. **Linearisation exactness (tier 1) and its error (measured).** `R(f; g) =
   g·s(f)` at the five exact frequencies for `g ∈ {−15, −6, +3, +6}`; the
   maximum error elsewhere is printed and recorded in this file, not asserted.
3. **Minimum-phase kernel (tier 1).** A cookbook peaking section is already
   minimum-phase: `minimumPhaseFromMagnitude(|H|)` reproduces its own phase
   within Shape A. A pure delay `e^{−jωD}` yields `φ_x = −ωD`, `τ_0 = D/fs`,
   zero swing after removal.
4. **The two-path pair (tier 1, the test this record exists for).**
   `a = 0.8` and `a = 1.25`, `D = 144` samples at 48 kHz: their magnitudes
   coincide bin for bin after offset (`|1 + a e^{−jθ}| = a|1 + e^{−jθ}/a|`);
   the first classifies **Boostable** with `S` within Shape A of 0, the
   second **NotMinimumPhase** with `S` within Shape A of `4·arcsin 0.8 =
   3.71 rad` (212°) against `S* = 1.85 rad` (106°) at its `D ≈ 19.1 dB`.
   Repeated at `a ∈ {0.5, 2}` (`S = 120°`) and `{0.25, 4}` (`S = 58°`, `D =
   4.4 dB`, `S* = 29°`) so shallow dips are covered.
5. **Coherence passes a coherent notch (tier 1).** `y = x∗h_comb + noise` at
   40 dB SNR through `DualFftEngine`: the 12 dB notch bin reads `γ² > 0.99`
   while the classifier reads NotMinimumPhase for `a > 1` — the two gates
   are shown to be independent on one fixture.
6. **Greedy placement (tier 1).** A single Gaussian bump in `r` of known
   centre and half-depth width: the candidate lands on the centre bin and
   `Q = fc/(f_2 − f_1)` to the grid's resolution; a bump inside the exclusion
   mask is not proposed.
7. **Gain solve (tier 3, narrowly).** Fixed `(fc, Q)` set and synthetic `r`;
   `numpy.linalg.solve(SᵀWS + λI, SᵀWr)` in the golden, Shape B; the
   collinear pair (`fc`, `Q` identical) resolves to equal halves of the
   unregularised single-filter gain; the isolated filter at `G_cap` shrinks
   by `0.1 dB ± 1e-9`.
8. **Whole pipeline (labelled regression lock).** A three-peak fixture: the
   `γ²/f`-weighted RMS residual after `autoEq(N = 3)` is below the input's,
   and the ghost equals `m + attenuation`-summed cascade exactly. Labelled a
   lock, not a proof.
9. **Refusals.** `fs ≤ 0`, `fc ≥ fs/2`, `Q ≤ 0`, mismatched span lengths,
   `N > number of trusted extrema`, and every-bin-untrusted all throw or
   return an empty result with a reason — never a curve of anything
   (`a-fixed-defect-returns-through-the-silent-fallback`).
10. **Guards.** `core_has_no_framework_deps` and
    `filter_design_has_no_polynomial_form` green with the new files and
    `gen_autoeq.py` scanned; counts read from the guards' own output.

## 10. Rejected options and what each would have cost

| Rejected | Cost that decided it |
|---|---|
| Global nonlinear optimisation (AutoEq SLSQP) | Unprovable without a frozen solver golden; blind to §1.2; no first suggestion (§3) |
| Pure greedy with per-filter gains (REW) | Adjacent filters fight; ghost misses by the overlap (§3) |
| Inverse-variance residual weights | 99:1 dominance; misfit, not noise, is the residual (§3, L6b §3) |
| Unweighted linear-frequency LS | Fits the top octave, starves the bottom three (§3) |
| Boost cap as the null gate | Cannot separate `a = 0.8` from `a = 1.25` (§4.4) |
| Fixed 90° excess-phase threshold | Misses every NMP dip shallower than ~7 dB (§4.4) |
| Ship without the phase test until L4c | Boosts into the one class of dip the feature exists to avoid (§4.4) |
| Gating cuts as well as boosts | Refuses harmless corrections; a wrong cut costs level, not headroom (§4.3) |
| All-pass in the EQ solver | Zero column in `S`; phase is V2's (§3) |
| Named X-curve / Harman presets | False conformance claim without the purchased table / verified source (§5) |
| A second minimum-phase implementation beside G10's | Two floors, two aliasing stories, one bug that surfaces as a phase offset (§4.3) |
| Breakpoints handed to FIR export | dB-level interpolation error on a high-Q peak (§7) |
