# Impulse-response decay analysis — decisions (lane L4b)

*2026-08-30. Sessions EP06 DOER (building) and EP06 THINKER (reviewing), on one
repository, in separate worktrees, measuring independently.*

Scope: energy-time curve, Schroeder backward integration, Lundeby truncation,
EDT / T20 / T30, and the clarity family C50 / C80 / D50. All of it inside
`core/`, consuming the `rta::ir::Deconvolution` that lane L4a produced.

**Read decision 2 before any other, and read the refusal in decision 6 before
shipping a number to an operator.** Everything else follows from those two.

## Provenance, stated once so it is never assumed

The definitions of EDT, T20, T30, C50, C80 and D50 live in **ISO 3382-1**,
which **nobody on this project has bought**. On 2026-08-30 the owner was asked
and deliberately deferred the purchase decision — *"ISO thì cần bàn thêm sau"* —
which is neither an unasked question nor an answered one; it is tracked in
`docs/HUMAN-QA-QUEUE.md`.

So every definition here is built from **open literature**: Schroeder (1965) for
backward integration, Lundeby et al. (1995) for truncation, and the published
source of `python-acoustics` 0.2.6, `pyrato`, and REW's documentation for how
the definitions are applied in practice.

**No document produced by this lane may say "per ISO 3382-1".** The rule is
chosen because it reverses cheaply in both directions: buying the standard later
adds clause citations beside numbers that already exist, whereas removing a
false claim of conformance means re-auditing everything that leaned on it. Lane
L4a paid this tuition once already, citing AES-2id — a specification about AES3
digital interfaces — for advice about sweeps (trap #16).

## 1. The pipeline, and where L4b begins

```
sweep -> capture -> deconvolve -> IR -> band filter -> square -> backward
                                  ^                                integrate
                                  |                                    |
                            L4b starts here                   truncate + correct
                                                                       |
                                                            fit slope -> EDT/T20/T30
                                                            ratio     -> C50/C80/D50
```

L4b consumes `rta::ir::Deconvolution` and does not re-derive it. That boundary
is also where this lane's golden vectors are cut — see decision 7.

## 2. Truncation is the main event, not a refinement

This is the decision every other one is downstream of, and it was not obvious
before it was measured. The intuition going in was that the interesting choice
would be *how to filter*. It is not, by an order of magnitude.

The Schroeder integral accumulates **every sample** of the record into its
plateau, including the samples that are pure noise after the room has stopped.
So the reading depends on how long the operator happened to leave the recorder
running. Measured on a synthetic decay with an exactly known T60, ensemble of 24
seeds, forward filtering, median T30 error:

| SNR | truncation | tail 2.5×T60 | tail 5.0×T60 | tail 7.5×T60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 45 dB | none | +428 % | +1812 % | +3264 % |
| 45 dB | Lundeby | +17.5 % | +19.5 % | +16.1 % |
| 55 dB | none | +7.7 % | +21.9 % | +205.7 % |
| 55 dB | Lundeby | +3.6 % | +4.4 % | +4.7 % |

**The decisive property is not the size of the improvement. It is the word
FLAT.** Untruncated, the answer is a function of a decision the operator made
for unrelated reasons — when to stop recording. Truncated, it is not. That is
the difference between a measurement and a coincidence.

Reproduce: `tools/probe_l4b_truncation.py`. The reviewing session built an
independent fixture and an **oracle** truncation — for a synthetic decay the
crossing point is known analytically, so nothing about it is estimated — and
measured the untruncated range as +365 % to +3254 % against this lane's +389 %
to +3286 %, with the oracle column flat at +0.3 % to +1.6 %. Two constructions,
built without reading each other, landing on the same mechanism.

### 2a. No truncation-bias figure may be quoted without its conditions

The first version of this survey reported "+13 % to +73 %" and that range was
wrong — not mis-measured, but meaningless, because the probe sized its buffer as
`max(2.0, 2.5·T60)` seconds, which is 5.0×T60 at one point of the grid and
2.5×T60 at every other. Its rows were never comparable, and a tail-length effect
was read as a filter-mode effect.

**Every truncation-bias number in this document carries its (SNR, tail length,
band).** A bare percentage is a number that will be re-used where it is false.

### 2b. The two capture-length rules are one decision

L4a decision 9 bounds capture length **from below**: the capture must outlast
the sweep by at least 1.5 × the reverberation time, or the decay is cut off
before it finishes and the answer reads short.

Decision 2 above bounds the same quantity **from above**: every noise-only
sample past the decay is energy the backward integral adds to the plateau.

They are one decision and belong in one place, because a later session reading
them apart will optimise one end and break the other. The resolution is not a
narrower capture window — **the upper bound is removed by truncating, not by
recording less**, which is precisely why Lundeby is the main event.

## 3. Lundeby is derived here, not transcribed

`pyrato`'s `intersection_time_lundeby` is the most complete open implementation
and it is **not** the source of the algorithm below.

The shallow reason: a public issue against that function reports a broken
convergence check. The reviewing session then read current `main` and found
convergence working, with a dead variable left behind — so the issue had gone
stale. **A public bug report has a date; code must be read at a commit.** That
lesson is now in `memory/`.

The real reason: transcribing an implementation means inheriting whichever of
its choices were accidents, and this lane would then have no way to tell an
accident from a decision. Every constant below is therefore named and defended,
and pyrato was consulted **afterwards**, as a check.

| constant | value | why |
|---|---|---|
| intervals per 10 dB | 5 | Sizes the smoothing window **from the measured decay rate**. A fixed millisecond window cannot be right at both ends — 30 ms is a third of a dry room's decay and a twentieth of a hall's. This is the same defect, in a different quantity, that L4a measured in its fixed 50 ms polarity window, which read a 50–71 Hz subwoofer as 46.9–18270 Hz. |
| standoff above noise | 10 dB | Keeps the regression out of the knee where decay and noise are comparable. The error there is **one-sided** — it flattens the slope, reading long — so it does not average out across bands. |
| late-decay fit span | 20 dB | Wide enough that slope error is small, narrow enough to stay above the knee. 20 dB below a 10 dB standoff needs 30 dB of usable range, which is where T30's dynamic-range requirement comes from rather than being asserted separately. |
| noise re-estimate margin | 10 dB **of decay** | See below. |
| convergence | 0.01 s, ≤ 30 iterations | Checked against the **previous** crossing point each pass. Written explicitly because the best-known reference shipped a version where that variable was assigned once and never updated, so the check could not fire. |

### 3a. The margin is decibels of decay, never a fraction of the buffer

An earlier draft placed the noise re-estimate margin at `0.10 × record length`.
It looks harmless. It is not: it ties the noise estimate to the record's length,
**reintroducing one level down the exact dependency decision 2 exists to
remove** — and reintroducing it where it is much harder to see.

It was caught by the reviewing session before it reached C++, and it was not
merely structural. At high ring-to-decay ratio the fitted slope is shallow, so
the two margins behave completely differently: under the buffer-fraction margin
one cell returned **+129.1 %**, under the decay margin the estimator **refuses**.
One line changed a confidently wrong answer into a declined one. The physics did
not move.

**When the margin does not fit inside the record, refuse — never fall back to a
fraction of the buffer.** A fallback there silently restores the dependency.

## 4. The filter-mode question is a ratio, and asking it by frequency is asking it wrong

Every reference band-pass filters the IR before integrating, and they disagree
about how. REW ships three modes and documents the trade-off as a contradiction:
time-reversed "greatly reduces the filter's own contribution" but "EDT figures
using Time-Reversed filters may not be valid"; zero-phase reduces it less "but
without significantly affecting early decay time".

A first survey across octave bands found all three modes agreeing within about
2 % and nearly concluded they were equivalent. **That agreement was an artefact
of where the survey looked.** Whether the mode matters is a question about how
the filter's own decay compares with the room's — and the octave survey never
got that comparison anywhere near unity.

### 4a. The axis is **B·T**, not a ratio of decay times

The obvious way to express it — filter's own T60 divided by the room's — was
measured by both sessions and **the two tables disagreed by exactly ×2 on every
row**. The cause was not a formula error on either side: one session designed
the band as `butter(4, btype='bandpass')` (8th order overall) and the other as
`butter(8, ...)` (16th order). Both measured their own filter correctly. They
were measuring different filters.

That is disqualifying for a shipped threshold. A decay ratio is **a property of
an implementation's filter order**, so a constant expressed in it means nothing
to anyone who designs their bands differently — including this project's own
future self.

**The bandwidth-time product B·T is immune to that**, because `B` is read off
the band table and `T` belongs to the room. It is also the quantity the
literature already uses: the classical validity floor for measuring a decay
through a band-pass is **B·T > 16**, and Jacobsen & Rindel's time-reversed
method is reported to lower that to about **4**.

| band | B (Hz) | B·T at T = 0.4 s | B·T at T = 1.2 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| octave 125 Hz | 88.4 | 35.4 | 106.1 |
| ⅓-octave 125 Hz | 28.9 | 11.6 | 34.7 |
| ⅓-octave 63 Hz | 14.6 | **5.8** | 17.5 |
| ⅓-octave 40 Hz | 9.3 | **3.7** | 11.1 |

This is the same move L4a made when it replaced a bandwidth constant with a box
on *measured* band edges: **gate on a quantity the measurement produces, never
on a constant internal to one implementation.**

### 4b. Forward filtering's EDT error is monotone in B·T, and it is physics

Ensemble of 24 seeds, SNR 55 dB, tail 2.5×T60, **oracle truncation — the
crossing is known analytically, so nothing whatever is estimated**:

| band, T60 | B·T | forward EDT | zero-phase EDT | time-reversed EDT |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ⅓-oct 125, 0.4 s | 11.6 | +68.0 % | +21.9 % | +27.0 % |
| ⅓-oct 40, 1.2 s | 11.1 | +48.0 % | +4.7 % | +6.9 % |
| ⅓-oct 63, 0.4 s | 5.8 | **+127.9 %** | +24.3 % | +11.0 % |
| ⅓-oct 40, 0.4 s | 3.7 | **+248.0 %** | +56.5 % | −7.4 % |

Forward filtering reports an early decay time **two and a half times the truth**
at B·T = 3.7, with the estimator removed from the experiment. That is the signal
path, not Lundeby. The trend is monotone in B·T and the two cells at B·T ≈ 11 —
different bands, different rooms — agree to within the scatter, which is what
makes B·T look like the governing variable rather than a coincidence.

Under Lundeby truncation the same cells refuse rather than answering (24 of 24
seeds at B·T ≤ 5.8). Both behaviours are the same fact: forward filtering does
not deliver a usable early decay time down here.

### 4c. B·T ≈ 4 is a floor for **every** mode, and that is the refusal line

At B·T = 3.7, T30 under oracle truncation reads +19.6 % (forward, 20 of 24
refusing), +45.8 % (zero-phase) and +16.3 % (time-reversed). Nothing is usable.
The literature's ~4 floor for the best available method is reproduced here rather
than assumed, and it lands where the measurement stops carrying information —
not where an algorithm stops coping.

**`B·T < 4` therefore refuses, for every filtering mode.** The gate is computed
from the band table and the fitted decay, so it is stateless and measurable, and
it does not depend on anyone's filter order.

### 4d. Decision: zero-phase, and what that forces

**`core/` filters zero-phase (forward-backward) for decay analysis.**

The argument is not that it wins everywhere. Above the B·T = 4 floor the picture
is split and the split is consistent across both truncation methods: zero-phase
is better on **T30**, time-reversed is better on **EDT**. Forward is disqualified
outright by 4b.

Zero-phase is chosen because **T30 is the headline number an operator acts on**,
and because it is never catastrophic anywhere inside the region this lane will
answer in. The cell where zero-phase is worst — B·T = 3.7, T30 +45.8 % — is
refused by 4c regardless of mode, so the choice is not being made on a cell that
ships.

Two consequences follow and both are load-bearing:

1. **Zero-phase filtering is non-causal, so it exists only offline.** It cannot
   run in the audio callback and it cannot run in a streaming bank. This settles
   decision 5 rather than merely agreeing with it.
2. **The IR must carry samples before the direct sound, or be padded.** See
   decision 5a; this is where the 38.7 dB comes from.

### 4e. What is NOT decided, and must not be quoted as if it were

- **Zero-phase versus time-reversed is not resolved cell by cell.** In most
  cells their medians differ by less than the inter-quartile spread. What is
  stable is the *sign* of the difference across both truncation methods and
  several B·T values — zero-phase ahead on T30, time-reversed ahead on EDT — and
  a consistent sign under a large spread is weaker evidence than a separated
  median. The decision in 4d rests on that, and on T30 being the number
  operators act on, not on a clean measured win.
- **REW's claim that time-reversed invalidates EDT is not reproduced.** Measured
  here, time-reversed is the *best* mode for EDT at low B·T (−7.4 % at B·T =
  3.7, against +56.5 % for zero-phase and +248 % for forward). The first survey
  that failed to reproduce it did so on octave bands where no mode differed, and
  that would have been the same mistake as 4a — so it was re-run across the B·T
  range where the modes do separate, and the non-reproduction held.

  It is still recorded as a **failure to reproduce, not a refutation.** REW's
  documentation does not state the B·T range its warning applies to, and this
  lane's scatter is wide. A sourced claim is not traded for an unsourced one.
- **The literature's 60–100 % filter bias for T60 = 0.4–1.2 s was not
  reproduced** and is not cited anywhere in this lane.
- **B·T = 16.** The classical validity floor is quoted above as the literature's
  figure and is NOT used as a threshold in shipped code. Only the ~4 floor was
  measured here, and only that one gates anything.

## 5. `FilterBank` is the wrong tool, and the reason is structural

`rta::dsp::FilterBank` is a **streaming meter** bank: `process()` returns `void`
and accumulates `meanSquare()` and `smoothed()`. It never exposes a band-filtered
time series, which is exactly what backward integration needs.

Three options were weighed.

| option | cost |
|---|---|
| Extend `FilterBank` to emit per-band samples | Changes a class that serves the SPL meter, with the IEC 61672 class-1 guards around it. An offline lane would be pulling risk into a live measurement path for a convenience. |
| Run the `Biquad` cascade directly from `sections(i)` | Cheap, and L4b owns its own filter state. But it inherits the causal, forward-only structure. |
| **A separate offline band-filter path (chosen)** | One more code path to keep aligned with the band table. |

Decision 4b makes the choice for us rather than leaving it to taste: zero-phase
filtering is non-causal, so **no streaming bank can express it**. The realtime
bank and the offline decay filter are not two implementations of one idea; they
are two different operations that happen to share a band table. They share
`OctaveBands` and `ButterworthDesign` and nothing else.

### 5a. The lead-in is worth 38.7 dB and is not optional

`filtfilt` pads its input at both edges (odd extension by default). Around a
direct sound sitting at sample 0, an odd extension reflects to a step of twice
the direct sound, and the filter rings on **that**. Measured directly on a unit
spike through a 125 Hz octave filter:

```
filtfilt(spike at sample 0)     total energy  24.70
filtfilt(spike at 200 ms)       total energy   0.0033
                                             +38.7 dB
```

Thirty-eight decibels of energy no room produced, landing exactly where EDT
reads. **Trimming an IR to begin at the direct sound is the ordinary thing to
do**, so this is not a probe curiosity — it is a hazard for any implementation
that trims and then filters zero-phase.

L4a reached the same conclusion from the opposite direction in `IrSpectrum.h`
("the window starts BEFORE t = 0, and that is not a detail"), for an unrelated
reason: the analysis pulse is symmetric about its peak. Two independent routes
to one requirement is the kind of agreement worth trusting.

`Deconvolution` already keeps the negative-time region, so the samples exist.
**L4b must not discard them before filtering**, and must refuse rather than
silently zero-pad when they are absent.

## 6. Where the answer is refused, and why the reason must say VARIANCE

One corner of the grid stays bad after everything above: **SNR 45 dB, T60 =
0.4 s, 125 Hz — T30 biased +16 % to +20 % with an inter-quartile spread of 12 to
20.** The bias is the size of the scatter, so no operator can act on either
number. Every other corner lands within a few percent.

The reviewing session's oracle run separates the two halves of that, and the
separation decides how the refusal must be **worded**:

| | with Lundeby | with the oracle |
|---|---|---|
| bias | +18…21 % | **+1.3 %** |
| IQR | 14–20 | **~11** |

- The **bias is estimator-limited**: give the estimator the true crossing and it
  nearly vanishes. A better estimator could reclaim it.
- The **scatter is physics-limited**: it survives the oracle. A short decay in a
  narrow band contains few independent realisations, and no algorithm creates
  information that is not there. Only more captures reduce it.

**So the refusal is recorded as a variance limit, not a bias limit.** Getting
this backwards is not cosmetic: a future session reading "bias" will set out to
improve Lundeby until the corner opens, which is an impossible task, and will
conclude the code is broken when it is the measurement that is empty.

What ships: a refusal carrying its reason and its numbers, in the same shape
L4a's `Polarity::Refusal` uses. `Unknown` with a direction beats a confident
number an operator will act on.

## 7. Golden vectors: cut at the IR, and specify noise as SNR

Two rules, both arrived at by the reviewing session refuting a claim this lane
made.

**(a) The golden commits IR SAMPLES, not parameters for C++ to regenerate.**
Today `core/tests/golden/ir.txt` is 777 bytes of parameters and expected
scalars, and `test_ir_golden.cpp` rebuilds the `Sweep` on the C++ side. That is
fine for testing the generator; it is wrong for L4b, because two known
Python/C++ asymmetries in the generator (`docs/HANDOFF.md`, the ⚠️ living
constraint) would then leak into decay numbers.

**The cut is at the IR, and saying so is part of the rule.** Committing samples
"at the input" without naming the layer would close the generator door and open
the deconvolver one. Whether `deconvolve` is right is already guarded by
`ir.txt` and `test_ir_golden.cpp`.

**(b) Noise is specified as SNR against the fixture's own signal, never as an
absolute amplitude.** An absolute noise line makes SNR depend on whatever peak
the signal happens to have — and the two pipelines differ by exactly that, 1.0
against 0.5012. Six decibels of SNR moves the truncation point, which changes
T30, and near a refusal threshold **changes whether the answer is a number at
all**. A golden that disagrees about the *kind* of result is worse than one that
disagrees about a value.

Two supporting rules: samples pass through float32 **before** Python computes
the expectations (`core/tests/support/Golden.h` already documents this
convention), and fixture parameters sit **far** from every refusal threshold —
a fixture standing on a gate is a coin-flip in CI, not a test.

### 7a. Who guards what

L4b's golden is deliberately blind to generator and deconvolver divergence. That
is only safe because `test_generator_sweep.cpp` and `test_ir_golden.cpp` guard
those layers — a contract between tests that nothing in the repository states.
Simplifying `ir.txt` "for tidiness" would remove L4b's fence with no guard going
red: trap #1, a guard that stops guarding in silence.

The contract is recorded here, and a **short** pointer back to this section goes
in each of those two test files. Short on purpose: a fact repeated in *n* places
is wrong in *n*−1 of them.

## 8. The artefact that reads as a room, inherited from L4a

L4a measured that linear deconvolution turns steady noise into a decaying,
progressively low-pass-filtered tail, with

    apparent RT60 ≈ 3·T / log10(f2/f1)

and for a 2 s sweep over three decades that is RT60 = 2.00 s, shortening with
frequency — from a measurement containing no room at all.

**L4b uses this to recognise the artefact and never to subtract it.** Subtracting
a modelled artefact from a real measurement means shipping the model's errors as
data; the mechanism is derived but the magnitude is only good to about 10 % over
the range tested, and the residual is unexplained. Recognition is the honest use.

## 9. What this record does not decide

- **Relative polarity's ρ thresholds.** L4a recorded them as one-source numbers
  and required re-derivation before any ship. Not done here; this record does
  not authorise them.
- **C50 / C80 / D50 tolerances.** The definitions are ratios and are
  scale-invariant, so they inherit decisions 2 and 4 without new constants — but
  no accuracy envelope has been measured for them yet.
- **Third-octave as a shipping resolution.** Decision 4's ratio table shows
  ⅓-octave at 40–63 Hz is where every mode struggles. Whether L4b offers those
  bands at all, and with what refusal, is not settled here.
- **Multi-slope decays.** Lundeby's truncation correction assumes a **single**
  exponential late decay. A coupled space or a hard rear wall breaks that
  assumption, and the correction then underestimates. Unmeasured.
