## 0. What L6a is for, in one paragraph

A transfer function tells an engineer whether the system is right. An SPL log
tells a promoter, a venue and sometimes a magistrate whether the show was legal.
They are different audiences with a different failure mode: a wrong transfer
function costs a bad-sounding show, a wrong SPL log costs a licence. So this
lane's design pressure is not accuracy in the DSP sense — the arithmetic is
trivial — it is **provenance**: every number the report prints must be
traceable to a stated weighting, a stated detector, a stated integration window,
a stated calibration, and a record of every second the instrument could not be
trusted. The lane that precedes it built meters that are correct and that
nothing has ever called; this lane wires them into the application, bounds their
memory, gives them a clock that is made of samples rather than of wall time, and
then writes down what they saw in a form somebody else can check.

## 1. Provenance: seven things station 1 corrected before any decision was made

Seven premises this lane opened with were wrong. Three of them would have
reached code. They are corrected here so that nobody re-derives them, in the
manner of `2026-09-06-multichannel-l6b.md` §1.

1. **The Meters dependency is satisfied in `core/` and nowhere else.** The
   master plan says L6a "waits on the Meters track"; report 002 says the meters
   landed. Both true, and neither says that
   `grep -rn "rta::meter\|rta/meter"` over the whole repository returns **16
   lines, every one of them under `core/`** — two headers, two sources, one
   guard script, two test files. `WeightingType` appears in three files, all
   `core/`. `measure::Snapshot` carries no broadband level, no weighting, no
   detector. L6a is not "logging on top of a working SPL meter"; it is
   **building the SPL meter into the application** and then logging it. This is
   the L5c situation ("`app/` had never called L2's dual-FFT engine") repeated.

2. **The weighting record names the wrong table.** IEC 61672-1:2013's
   **Table 2 is "Acceptance limits for deviations of directional response from
   the design goal"**; the frequency-weighting tolerances are in **Table 3,
   "Frequency weightings and acceptance limits"** — both design goal and
   acceptance limits in the one table. `docs/dsp/2026-08-27-weighting-and-meters.md:41`
   and `core/tests/check_no_conformance_claim.cmake:18,61` all say "Table 2
   tolerance envelope". The *substance* of the open item is unchanged — the
   numbers are still paywalled and no Class claim may be made — but the table
   number is wrong in three places and a purchaser acting on it would buy
   against the wrong reference. Amendment noted in the weighting record; the
   guard's comment is code and is left for the lane that next touches it.

3. **Clause numbers moved between IEC 61672-1 Ed 1 (2002) and Ed 2 (2013).**
   Time-weightings F and S are **5.8** in Ed 2 (5.7 in Ed 1); overload
   indication **5.11** (was 5.10); under-range **5.12** (was 5.11); C-weighted
   peak **5.13** (was 5.12); display **5.18** (was 5.15). Any citation without
   an edition year is a coin flip, and at least one well-ranked page
   (`sonavyx.com`) cites "5.6" for time weighting, which is Level linearity.
   **Every clause this record cites is Ed 2 (2013) unless it says otherwise.**

4. **Ln is not in IEC 61672-1 at all, and "L90" on its own is not a quantity.**
   The project already knew the first half (the weighting record calls Ln "a
   convention this project must pick"). What station 1 adds is that Ln *is*
   standardised — in **ISO 1996-1:2016 clause 3.1.3, "N percentage exceedance
   level"** — and that the standard's own notation is
   **`L_AF95,1h`**: frequency weighting, time weighting, N, interval. A readout
   that says "L90" names neither the detector that produced it nor the window it
   covers, and two instruments printing "L90" are not necessarily measuring the
   same thing.

5. **REW's API is not read-only.** L6b §10 records REW as binding `127.0.0.1`
   with no password, which is right, and the owner's 2026-09-06 ruling took the
   *bind* from REW. But REW's own API page states that "PUT and POST are also
   supported by default for most endpoints" and warns "there are no delete
   confirmations". So this project's read-only-plus-localhost posture is
   **strictly more conservative than the precedent it cites**, not equal to it.
   Worth saying out loud, because a later session reading "REW model" could
   reasonably conclude that writes were always intended. The remote-API record
   written in parallel (PR #11 §9) reaches the same posture from its own
   research and goes further, putting a `Host`-header allowlist *above* the
   bind.

6. **IEC 61252 has a new edition, and it specifies no criterion.** **Edition
   2.0 was published 2025-10** and cancels and replaces 1993 + A1:2000 +
   A2:2017; there was no "2017 confirmation" (2017 was Amendment 2). It is a
   technical revision — C-weighted peak is now **mandatory** (cl. 4.18), F and
   S are specified (cl. 4.14), the range is at least 67–137 dB over
   20 Hz–8 kHz, the accuracy grade is class 2 of IEC 61672-1:2013, and
   non-equal-energy exchange rates are promoted from "permitted as a display
   conversion" to first-class specified quantities. And the premise the lane
   opened with — "85 dB(A) / 8 h from IEC" — is false: clauses 3.5–3.7 define
   criterion duration, criterion level and criterion exposure **parametrically**,
   with Note 1 to 3.7 saying the criterion exposure "is typically chosen to be a
   legal limit. Such limits vary between jurisdictions and are subject to
   change." The only 85 dB in IEC 61252 is the informative note that
   `1 Pa²h ≈ 85 dB`. **There is no defensible standard default**; every dose
   parameter is a setting.

7. **"3 dB exchange" means `q = 10` exactly, not `10·log10(2)` and not
   `2^(ΔL/3)`.** This session's own first draft of §7 had it wrong in all three
   directions. IEC 61252 Ed 2 Formula (7) uses exponent `0,1`; ANSI S1.25-1991
   clause 4.7 fixes `q = 10` for the 3 dB rate by fiat while writing the others
   as `Q/log 2`; and NIOSH's own published `TWA = 10.0·log(D/100) + 85` inverts
   to the same thing. The `2^(ΔL/3)` reading is IEC's *other* formula — the
   non-equal-energy one (cl. 3.10, Formula (8)) — evaluated at `Q = 3`. IEC's
   Note 3 to 3.10 calls the two "equivalent"; **they are not**, and §7 puts a
   number on the gap.

## 2. Decision: the logged unit is a **block**, its clock is a sample count, and short-term and long-term metrics are recorded differently within it

**Decision.** The logger emits one **block** per `blockSamples` of the
measurement channel, where `blockSamples = lround(interval * fs)` fixed at
session start. A block carries, at minimum:

```
blockIndex        uint64    0,1,2,...   (the clock; no wall time in the arithmetic)
blockSamples      uint32    the divisor every mean in this block used
sumSquares        double    the energy; Leq of the block is 10*log10(sumSquares/blockSamples)
maxFastDb         float     MAX of the F-weighted detector WITHIN the block
maxSlowDb         float     MAX of the S-weighted detector WITHIN the block
peakDb            float     10*log10(max p^2) WITHIN the block  (C-weighted per IEC; see below)
                            NOTE 2026-09-18: the OVERLOAD RUN is read off the
                            RAW hop, not this C-weighted stream -- amendment A3
flags             uint32    overload | underRange | dropped | calibrationInvalid
```

> **Amendment A1 (2026-09-18, station 4 Wave 0) — the field list above is
> one field and one flag short of what shipped.** See
> [§15 Amendment 2026-09-18](#15-amendment-2026-09-18-what-station-4-wave-0-measured-and-changed)
> for the block as built: `droppedSamples uint32` and a fifth flag `gap`. The
> list above is left standing because it is what the decision was made from.

Three choices inside that, each argued:

**The clock is samples, not milliseconds.** `AnalysisThread::publishIfDue()`
throttles on `juce::Time::getMillisecondCounter()` with
`kMinPublishIntervalMs = 50` — a *floor*, not a period, so publishes are jittery
and wall-clock-derived. A log sampled off the publish path would have rows whose
integration windows do not tile the session, which makes the energy sum in §3
wrong by however much the jitter was. Seconds are **derived and reported**
(`blockSamples / fs`), never assumed — the same discipline
`docs/dsp/2026-09-05-mtw-l3.md` adopted for MTW integration seconds. A wall
clock is still recorded, once per block, as *metadata for the human*, and is
never an input to any mean.

**Short-term metrics are max-held within the block; long-term are integrated.**
This is not an invention: Smaart SPL's guide states it outright (v9.1 p. 33) —
"For 'short term' metrics (Peak, SPL Fast and SPL Slow), the **highest level
reached during the logging interval** is recorded so that no maximum values are
missed. 'Long term' metrics (Leq and Exposure) are logged at their present
value." A logger that samples every metric instantaneously at the block boundary
silently discards the loudest moment of every block, which is the one moment a
complaint will be about.

**Argued against the obvious: "log the detector output every 100 ms and compute
everything later."** That is what `meter::Leq` already does internally, it is
the simplest thing, and it is wrong for three reasons that compound.
(a) It is not composable: 100 ms detector samples do not give an exact Leq over
a minute, because an exponentially-weighted mean square is not the arithmetic
mean of the signal's energy over the window — only a *sum of squares* is.
(b) It grows without bound — 2.1973 MiB per 8 h channel at 10 Hz, forever, in a
`push_back` inside a `noexcept` function (§C3 of the research).
(c) It answers the wrong question: ISO 1996-1 clause 3.1.6 and IEC 61672-1
clause 3.10's Note 3 both say time weighting is **not** involved in determining
Leq, so a log built out of time-weighted samples cannot produce a conforming Leq
at any window length. The block keeps *both*: a raw sum of squares for Leq, and
the detector maxima for Lmax.

**Default interval: 1 s, settable.** The market does not agree — Smaart SPL
defaults to **3 s**, 10EaZy writes **once per minute**, the Swiss cantonal rules
record **LAeq,5min every five minutes**, VLAREM registers **LAeq,60min**. One
second is the smallest interval every one of those divides into, so a 1 s log
can reconstruct any of them exactly by §3, and the reverse is false. The cost of
1 s over 3 s is a factor of three in file size, quantified in §4.

## 3. Decision: everything longer than a block is an **energy sum over blocks**, recomputed, never a running subtraction

**Decision.** For any window of blocks `i ∈ W` (a fixed reporting period, a
sliding compliance window, or the whole session):

```
Leq(W) = 10*log10( Σ_{i∈W} sumSquares_i / Σ_{i∈W} blockSamples_i ) + offsetDb
Lmax(W) = max over i∈W of maxFastDb_i            (and the S variant)
Lpeak(W) = max over i∈W of peakDb_i
L_E(W) = Leq(W) + 10*log10( T_W / T_0 ),  T_0 = 1 s
```

This is exact, not an approximation, and the identity is **already pinned in
`core/`** by `test_leq.cpp`'s "Leq of a piecewise signal is the duty-weighted
energy sum". The SEL relation is IEC 61672-1 **clause 3.12 Equation (4)** with
`E_0 = p_0^2 T_0 = (20 µPa)^2 × 1 s`, and Equation (6) is its inverse.

**Argued against the obvious: keep a running sum and subtract the departing
block.** That is the O(1) trick every sliding-window implementation reaches for.
**An earlier draft of this record rejected it for accumulated rounding, and that
reason does not survive its own arithmetic** — the number is recorded here
because a rejection whose stated reason is wrong is worse than no rejection.
Each add/subtract on a sum that stays near the window value `W` rounds by about
`eps·W`, so after `N` updates the relative error is bounded by `N·2^-53`:

| run | updates `N` | relative bound | in dB |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 h session, 1 s blocks | 28 800 | `3.2e-12` | `1.4e-11 dB` |
| 7 days, 1 s blocks | 604 800 | `6.7e-11` | `2.9e-10 dB` |
| **1 year at 10 Hz** | 315 576 000 | `3.5e-08` | **`1.5e-07 dB`** |

Even the year-long 10 Hz case is **six orders of magnitude** under the 0.1 dB
the display shows. Rounding is not the reason.

**The real reason is that the window is not a FIFO.** A block carries validity
`flags` (§2), and a block's membership can change *after* it was added: an
overload latch or a `calibrationInvalid` verdict can retire a block that is
already inside the window, the operator can change the window length mid-show,
and a segment roll can make the departing block a different object from the one
that entered. A running subtraction is only correct if the value removed is
bit-identically the value added and nothing in between reconsidered it — and
none of those three conditions holds here. The recompute has no such precondition
because it reads the current membership every time.

> **Amendment A2 (2026-09-18, station 4 Wave 0) — "an overload latch ... can
> retire a block" is NOT what shipped, and the difference changes every
> published Leq during a loud show.** The shipped rule is
> **`CalibrationInvalid` alone excludes**; `Overload` **includes**. See
> [§15 Amendment 2026-09-18](#15-amendment-2026-09-18-what-station-4-wave-0-measured-and-changed).
> The sentence above is left standing because the *reason* it gives for the
> recompute survives intact: membership is still mutable (a calibration
> verdict still retires a block, the operator can still change the window
> length, a segment roll still changes object identity), so a running
> subtraction is still wrong. Only the list of flags that retire a block is
> narrower than this paragraph implies.

The cost of that correctness is nothing: `N` is small by construction — a
15-minute window at 1 s blocks is **900 additions**, a 60-minute window
**3 600**, evaluated at most a few times a second — and summing 900 doubles has
a relative error bound of `900 × 2^-53 ≈ 1.0e-13`, i.e. `4.3e-13 dB`.

**What this decision buys the rest of the lane:** the log file *is* the data
model. The report, the CSV, the web viewer's history strip, the Ln table and
the dose all read the same blocks. There is no second accumulator to disagree
with the first.

## 4. Decision: the history ring is bounded by a declared span, allocated once, and stores numbers rather than pixels

**Decision.** One ring of blocks per logged channel, sized at session start from
an explicit **log span** setting, allocated once and never grown — the rule
`platform/`'s capture rings already follow ("rings allocated once forever",
report 002). The file is the unbounded record; the ring is the in-memory window
the display and the sliding-window alarm read.

Sizes. **The §2 block is 36 bytes of fields** —
`uint64 + uint32 + double + 3×float + uint32` = `8 + 4 + 8 + 12 + 4` — and
**40 bytes as laid out**, because the `double` forces 4 bytes of padding after
the leading `uint64 + uint32`. The ring is sized on the laid-out figure, 40 B;
an earlier draft of this table said 24 B and was 1.67× optimistic:

| span | 1 s blocks | bytes | | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 h | 3 600 | 144 000 | 0.137 MiB | a set |
| 8 h | 28 800 | 1 152 000 | 1.099 MiB | a show plus load-in |
| 24 h | 86 400 | 3 456 000 | 3.296 MiB | a festival day |
| 7 d | 604 800 | 24 192 000 | 23.07 MiB | at the edge; a monitoring install, not a show |

Against `meter::Leq`'s present unbounded **2.1973 MiB per 8 h and still
growing**, a declared 24 h ring at **3.296 MiB** is three times the span for
1.5× the memory, and finite. The 7-day figure is the one to notice: 23 MiB is
affordable but it is no longer a rounding error beside `platform/`'s 16 MB of
capture rings, so the log span belongs in the settings the operator sees rather
than in a constant.

**Argued against the obvious: "just keep everything in memory, it is only a few
megabytes."** It is a few megabytes *for a show*. The same code on a permanent
noise monitor is an out-of-memory in a `noexcept` function, which is
`std::terminate` — the application disappears, and on a compliance install the
thing that disappears is the evidence. A bound that is declared is a bound the
operator can be told about; a bound that is "however much RAM there is" is a
crash with a date on it.

**History stores numbers, not pixels** — adopted verbatim from
`docs/dsp/2026-08-29-display-layer-l5c.md` §7.2, which established the rule
after reading how OSM bakes colour into stored points (raw dB discarded, capped
at 51 rows with a `//TODO`) and how Friture's pixel ring buffer is as deep as
the widget is wide, so a resize destroys the history. The SPL strip is the same
class of object as the spectrograph scroller and inherits the same rule without
re-deriving it: blocks are stored, decimation and colour happen at draw.

**Ring-full behaviour is `roll`, never `wrap-silently`.** When the ring fills,
the oldest block leaves memory — it is already on disk — and the *session* does
not end. The file rolls to a new segment at a declared size so no single file
grows past what a text editor can open. The report states the segment list.

## 5. Decision: Ln comes from a fixed-width histogram, 0.1 dB bins, and a percentile outside the span is reported absent

**Decision.** A `core/` type accumulates the **time-weighted** level (per ISO
1996-1 clause 3.1.3, Ln *is* a time-weighted quantity, unlike Leq) into a
fixed-width histogram at the detector sampling rate:

```
w      = 0.1 dB        binCount = 2000        baseDb = configurable (default -20.0)
span   = [baseDb, baseDb + 200.0) dB
counts = uint32[2000], plus TWO separate counters: belowSpan, aboveSpan
memory = 2002 * 4 = 8 008 B = 7.82 KiB, allocated once, constant for any
         session length
Ln     = percentileLevelDb over bin CENTRES, the project's existing convention
         (linear interpolation between order statistics, numpy 'linear'),
         unchanged from docs/dsp/2026-08-27-weighting-and-meters.md
```

**The shape is copied deliberately from a Class-1 instrument, not invented.**
The Larson Davis 831/LxT SDK headers define
`NUM_STAT_BINS 2000` — "the number of level bins of **0.1 dB resolution** used
for Ln percentile calculations" — with `NUM_SLM_LN_BINS = NUM_STAT_BINS + 2`
(the over and under bins), an `m_nBaseDB` field, and `m_nLnTable` holding
**counts, not samples**. Adopting 2000 bins at 0.1 dB with a movable base and
two out-of-span counters means the one commercial data model anybody can read
and this one have the same shape, and the 200 dB span it implies covers every
level any calibration offset can put on screen.

**The bin width is derived, not surveyed.** Quantising every sample to its bin
centre is a monotone map that moves each value by at most `w/2`. A monotone map
commutes with sorting, so each order statistic moves by at most `w/2`; the
project's Ln is `v[lo] + frac·(v[hi] − v[lo])`, a convex combination of two
order statistics with `frac ∈ [0,1]`, so

```
|Ln_binned − Ln_exact| ≤ (1−frac)·w/2 + frac·w/2 = w/2
```

**exactly, for every n, every distribution and every sample count.** At
`w = 0.1 dB` the bound is **0.05 dB** — half the project's own dB display
resolution (CLAUDE.md: "dB keeps one decimal"), and equal to the semi-range
IEC 61672-2 clause 6.21 and IEC 61672-3 clause 4.2 both assign to a 0.1 dB
display's own resolution uncertainty. This is a bound that carries its
justification from outside any grid it was measured on, which is what
`memory/a-threshold-read-off-a-grid-is-that-grids-floor.md` asks of a constant
before it ships.

**Counter width is provably sufficient:** `2^32 − 1 = 4 294 967 295` samples at
10 per second is `429 496 729.5 s = 13.62 years` of continuous logging before a
single bin can overflow.

**A percentile that lands outside the span is absent, not clamped.** If the
number of samples below the span exceeds the rank being asked for, the answer is
"below −20.0 dB", reported as an absence with a reason, never as `−20.0`. This
is `memory/a-placeholder-for-an-absent-result-erases-its-state.md` applied
before it can bite: an L90 printed as exactly the bottom of the span reads as a
measurement and is a confession that the span was wrong.

**Argued against the obvious: keep every sample and compute the exact
percentile.** That is what `Leq::percentileDb` does today, and reading its code
is the argument against it. It copies the history into a vector, sorts it, and
then calls `percentileLevelDb`, **which copies and sorts it again** — two
allocations and two `std::sort`s of the whole history per percentile. Asking for
L10, L50 and L90 once over an 8 h session is six sorts of 288 000 doubles and
about 13.2 MiB of allocation churn, repeated at whatever rate the UI asks. And
the exactness it buys is not exactness about anything: the quantity being sorted
is a detector reading whose own uncertainty is at least the 0.05 dB of the
display it will be shown on, and an exact percentile of it does not compose —
merging two sessions' Ln means re-sorting both, while merging two histograms is
`a[i] += b[i]`.

**What the readout must say.** Following ISO 1996-1 clause 3.1.3's own notation,
the label carries all four parts: `L_AF90,15min`, never `L90`. The existing
project convention (Fast, 100 ms sampling, linear interpolation) stands
unchanged and is now *named in the label* rather than only in a doc.

## 6. Decision: the alarm compares a windowed Leq, and the number it reports is **remaining headroom**, not a colour

**Decision.** An alarm is configured as `(quantity, window T, limit L_lim)`,
where the quantity is an **Leq over a window**, never an instantaneous level.
The alarm's own state is a two-state latch on the windowed value — active while
`Leq(W) > L_lim`, clear otherwise — and this project ships **no invented
hysteresis and no invented debounce**. Alongside the state, and more usefully
than it, the alarm publishes a closed-form number.

**The headroom identity.** With window length `T`, elapsed `t` (`0 ≤ t < T`),
`L_t` the Leq over the elapsed part and `L_lim` the limit, the energy already
spent is `t·10^(L_t/10)` and the whole window's budget is `T·10^(L_lim/10)`. The
highest constant level that can be run for the remaining `T − t` without the
window exceeding the limit is

```
L_allow = 10*log10( ( T·10^(L_lim/10) − t·10^(L_t/10) ) / (T − t) )
```

Checks, all exact: if `L_t = L_lim` throughout then `L_allow = L_lim`; at
`t = T/2` with `L_t = L_lim − 10` the numerator is `0.95·T·10^(L_lim/10)` and
`L_allow = L_lim + 10*log10(1.9) = L_lim + 2.7875360 dB`. If the bracket is
**≤ 0** the window is already lost, and that is a *fact about the arithmetic*,
not a threshold — it is reportable as such and is the honest form of "you cannot
fix this by turning down now".

**Argued against the obvious: a red/amber/green traffic light with margins.**
Every competitor ships one and **not one of them publishes a margin.** Smaart
SPL's alarms have exactly two configurable fields, `Level (dB)` and `Duration`,
and `Duration` is *not* a debounce — the guide (v9.1 p. 32) says it "sets the
amount of time, in seconds, that the associated meter will flash a visual
warning when the alarm is triggered", i.e. post-fire flash time. Its traffic
light is "customize the Red, Yellow and Green color thresholds", entirely
user-defined with no published default. 10EaZy's amber is a closed prediction
algorithm whose only documented trigger is qualitative ("if the average level is
approaching the dB limit"). So a margin shipped here would be **originated by
this project**, and `memory/a-threshold-read-off-a-grid-is-that-grids-floor.md`
is about exactly that: a number with an honest provenance label is still a
number nobody can check. `L_allow` needs no margin to be meaningful, in the same
way L6b's phase agreement `R` needed no threshold to be meaningful. The
operator's own amber is then a setting applied *to `L_allow`* — "warn me when I
have less than 3 dB of headroom left" is a sentence an operator can reason
about, and it is theirs, not ours.

**Two numbers that look like amber margins and are not.** The UK Noise Council
Pop Code clause 4.12 suggests a 1-minute LAeq control limit "typically some
2-3 dB(A) above the 15 minute value", and Flanders' VLAREM II art. 5.32.2.2bis
§2 1° deems an `LAeq,60min ≤ 100 dB(A)` limit satisfied if `LAeq,15min` does not
exceed **102 dB(A)**. Two independent jurisdictions converging on +2..3 dB is
the strongest external evidence in the whole research pass — but it is evidence
about **how much a shorter-integration proxy may sit above a longer-integration
limit**, not about where a warning colour goes. Reusing it as an amber margin
would be the category error the memory file exists to prevent. It is instead the
justification for §6's *proxy* feature: when the regulated window is long, the
app may also show a shorter window, and the offset between the two is the
operator's setting with these two published precedents printed beside the field.

**Why flicker is not a problem worth inventing hysteresis for.** It is a
property of the quantity, not of the comparator. Smaart ships a single threshold
and does not flicker because what it compares is already a logged, integrated
value at a 3 s interval; 10EaZy compares an Leq over at least 3 minutes. A
15-minute sliding Leq changes by at most a bounded amount per block by
construction. Integration is doing the work hysteresis would otherwise do, and
adding hysteresis on top would be a second undocumented constant defending
against a problem the first one already solved.

**What the alarm records.** Every transition is a **marker** in the log, with
its block index, its direction, the quantity, the window and the value. The
markers are what the report's violation table and the web viewer's strip both
read. Following Smaart's taxonomy, the marker kinds are `alarm`, `overload`,
`note` and `reset`.

## 7. Decision: dose is one formula whose exchange rate is a per-preset *number*, two accumulators run at once, and peak never enters the integral

**Decision.** A dose accumulator is parameterised by
`(L_c, T_c, q, L_threshold)` and accumulates over the §2 blocks:

```
D% = (100 / T_c) · Σ_blocks  t_i · 10^((L_i − L_c)/q)
                             terms with L_i < L_threshold contribute exactly 0
```

`q` is a **base-10 denominator carried as a number by the preset**, never a
boolean "3 dB or 5 dB". This is IEC 61252:2025 clause 3.9 Formula (7) when
`q = 10`, and its clause 3.10 Formula (8) — `10^((L−L_c)·lg2/Q)` — when
`q = Q/log10(2)` for a declared exchange rate `Q`. The equivalent allowed-time
form that OSHA's own Appendix A uses, `D = 100·Σ Cᵢ/Tᵢ` with
`Tᵢ = T_c·2^((L_c−Lᵢ)/Q)`, is algebraically the same thing; this record ships
the energy form because it accumulates directly off §3's block energies.

**Two accumulators run simultaneously**, each with its own four settings. This
is not a convenience: the Larson Davis 831/LxT defines `NUM_SLM_DOSES = 2` with
a per-dose `{thresholdLevelDB, exchangeRate, criterionTimeHours,
criterionLevelDB}`, and it is what makes Smaart SPL's `Exposure O` and
`Exposure N` columns exist side by side in one log. A single dose setting cannot
produce that log, and it forces a wrong threshold onto whichever preset loses.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **NIOSH REL** (accumulator A default) | 85 dB(A) | 8 h | **9.9657843** (`= 3/log10 2`) | **80 dB(A)** | NIOSH 98-126 §1.1.1 p.1, `T(min) = 480/2^((L−85)/3)`, "where 3 = the exchange rate", and Table 1-1 p.2; §1.3.3 p.4 requires all levels "from 80 to 140 dBA" to be integrated, which is where the 80 dB(A) threshold comes from and why Table 1-1 starts at 80 rather than at the 85 REL |
| **OSHA PEL** (accumulator B default) | 90 dB(A) | 8 h | **16.6096404** (`= 5/log10 2`) | **90 dB(A)** for PEL dose, **80 dB(A)** for the hearing-conservation dose | 29 CFR 1910.95 (a) requires A-weighting and **Slow**; the dose is computed against **Table G-16a** per Appendix A (mandatory) **I(1)(i)** — *not* against Table G-16, which is the body's permissible-exposure table — and Appendix A I(2) gives `TWA = 16.61 log10(D/100) + 90` |

**Provenance correction, because the first draft of this section argued from the
wrong table.** It used a six-row list — 8 h/85, 4 h/88, 2 h/91, 60 min/94,
30 min/97, 15 min/100 — and called it "NIOSH's duration table". It is not. That
is the **CDC 2016 bulletin's summary**: six selected rows that happen to sit on
the 3 dB grid. NIOSH's own table is **Table 1-1, printed page 2 of DHHS (NIOSH)
98-126**, "Combinations of noise exposure levels and durations that no worker
exposure shall equal or exceed" — **51 rows in 1 dB steps from 80 dBA to a
final `130–140 → <1 sec`**, in Hours / Minutes / Seconds columns. Station 1 also
recorded that the primary "is a scanned image with no text layer" and argued
from the bulletin instead. **That reason is refuted**: the archived copy at
`web.archive.org/web/2020/https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/docs/98-126/pdfs/98-126.pdf`
is **born-digital** (`/Author NIOSH`, `/Creator Adobe InDesign CC 2014`) with a
real text layer. It has now been read, and the research file cites pages.

**Argued against the obvious: "3 dB exchange rate" and "energy" are the same
thing, so set `q = 10` for NIOSH and move on.** Reading the primary refutes it —
and puts the inconsistency somewhere different from where the first draft put
it. **Chapter 1 contains two tables that do not use the same `q`:**

- **Table 1-1 (p.2)** is generated by the formula printed above it in §1.1.1
  (p.1), `T (min) = 480 / 2^((L−85)/3)`, followed by the words *"where 3 = the
  exchange rate"*. That is `q = 3/log10(2) = 9.9657843`.
- **Table 1-2 (p.3)**, "Daily noise dose as an 8-hr TWA", converts dose to TWA
  by its own printed footnote `*TWA = 10 × Log(D/100) + 85` (the in-text form on
  p.2 writes `10.0`). That is **`q = 10` exactly**. Its last row is the proof and
  needs no interpretation: **32,500,000 % → 140.1 dBA**, and
  `10·log10(325000) + 85 = 140.1188`.

**So the inconsistency is Table 1-1 against Table 1-2, inside one chapter of one
document** — not "the table against the formula", which is how the first draft
phrased it, and which was wrong in both directions: Table 1-1 *agrees* with its
own formula, and it is Table 1-2's formula that differs. The size of the
disagreement is a closed form rather than a single figure:

```
D(q = 10) / D(q = 3/log10 2)  =  10^(−0.00034333 · ΔL),    ΔL = L − 85
```

| level | ΔL | `q = 10` reads this much low |
|---|---|---|
| 100 dB(A) | 15 | 1.1788 % |
| 115 dB(A) | 30 | 2.3437 % |
| 130 dB(A) | 45 | 3.4949 % |
| **140 dB(A)** — the top of NIOSH's own 80–140 measurement range (§1.3.3) | 55 | **4.2549 %** |

**The first draft's "up to 1.18 %" was the endpoint of the wrong table**: it is
the value at 100 dBA, where the bulletin's six-row summary stops. Against the
real table the gap reaches **4.2549 %**. Seen from the other side the same fact
is `2^10/10^3 = 1.024` at `ΔL = 30`.

**This record ships the value that reproduces Table 1-1** (`q = 9.9657843`),
because Table 1-1 is the artefact an inspector reads and it agrees with its own
printed formula; it prints the TWA with NIOSH's own `10·log10(D/100) + 85` and
states beside it that the two tables imply different exchange constants. Which
one ships is still a question for the owner (§13 Q4).

**OSHA is the same shape, not the mirror image.** The first draft claimed OSHA
was "merely rounded" while NIOSH "disagreed in substance", and that asymmetry
does not exist. OSHA's dose is computed against **Table G-16a** (Appendix A
I(1)(i)), whose footnote formula `T = 8/2^((L−90)/5)` is exact and whose printed
durations are **also rounded** — at 81 dBA the exact value is **27.857618 h**
and the table prints **27.9**. Both regulators print an exact formula and a
table rounded off it.

**And both tables contain arithmetic errata, which is the real reason the
fixtures below are shaped the way they are.** Recomputing every row against the
document's own formulas turns up three cells in 98-126 that do not reconcile,
all three confirmed as genuinely printed (rendered and read, not an extraction
artefact):

| table | row | printed | from the document's own formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-1 | 99 dB(A) | 18 min **59** sec | 18 min **53.93** sec |
| 1-2 | 50,000 % | **102.0** dBA | **111.99** dBA — a single-digit substitution (`1` → `0` in the tens place, 112.0 → 102.0), not a transposition; the neighbours 45,000 → 111.5 and 60,000 → 112.8 are right, so 102.0 breaks monotonicity |
| 1-2 | 26,000,000 % | **139.0** dBA | **139.15** dBA, where every other row lands within 0.05 |

The document **never says the tables are rounded** — there is no such statement
and Table 1-1 has no footnote at all. The rounding is evident from the numbers
and is *not even internally consistent*: most rows are round-to-nearest in the
smallest printed unit, but 124 dBA (exact 3.516 s, printed 3) and 127 dBA (exact
1.758 s, printed 1) are truncated.

**Closed forms the acceptance tests assert** — all exact, no golden vector:

| fixture | expected |
|---|---|
| `L = L_c`, `T = T_c` | `D = 100 %` exactly, for every `q` |
| `L = L_c + Q`, `T = T_c/2`, with `q = Q/log10 2` | `D = 100 %` exactly |
| `L = L_c − Q`, `T = 2·T_c`, same `q` | `D = 100 %` exactly |
| half at `L_c − Q` and half at `L_c + Q`, each `T_c/2` | `D = 100·(0.25 + 1.0) = 125 %` |
| the **exact** duration from each regulator's own footnote formula, at every 1 dB step | `D = 100 %` to float tolerance. This asserts the **formula**, which is exact and is what the code implements |
| every **printed** row of NIOSH Table 1-1 and OSHA Table G-16a | `\|D − 100 %\| ≤ 100·r / T_exact`, where `T_exact` is the formula's value and `r` is **that row's own printed resolution** — for Table 1-1, 1 min on a row whose smallest printed unit is minutes and 1 s on a row printed in seconds; for Table G-16a, whose durations are hours to three significant figures, **one unit in that row's last printed decimal place** (0.1 h at `81 → 27.9`, 0.001 h at `130 → 0.031`). The bound is **derived from the printing, not chosen**: 0.066 % at 80 dBA, 0.111 % at 100 dBA, 11.3 % at 120 dBA, 90.3 % at 129 dBA — a duration printed to the nearest second carries no useful precision once it is under two seconds. Run over all 50 single-level rows it holds everywhere **except 99 dBA**, which is the erratum above — so the same bound that tolerates the rounding also **detects the typo**, and the fixture pins that one row as a known-erratum exclusion rather than silently widening for it |
| NIOSH Table 1-2's last row | `TWA(32,500,000 %) = 140.1188` → prints 140.1 dBA, pinning **that** table at `q = 10` |
| all blocks below `L_threshold` | `D = 0 %`, and the *time* spent below threshold is reported separately, never folded into the dose |
| `q = 10` vs `q = 9.9657843` | the ratio `10^(−0.00034333·ΔL)`: `2^10/10^3 = 1.024` at `ΔL = 30`, and **4.2549 % low at 140 dB(A)** |

**`L_EX,8h` is energy and must never take `q`.**
`L_EX,8h = L_Aeq,T + 10·log10(T / 8h)` is IEC 61252 Ed 2 Formula (5), and its
Note 6 says Formula (2) is identical to Directive 2003/10/EC's daily noise
exposure level. Displayed beside an OSHA 5 dB dose it will not agree; that is a
property of two definitions, not a bug, and the report says so in words next to
both numbers.

**Projected dose is a vendor convention and is labelled one.**
`D_proj = D_now · (T_c / T_elapsed)`. Station 1 read the whole of IEC 61252 Ed 2
clause 3 (3.1–3.11) and the whole of ANSI S1.25-1991's definitions and sections
1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7.0–7.6: **the word "projected" does not appear in either.**
Three independent vendors (Castle Group, Larson Davis, Faber Acoustical) define
it identically and none cites a clause. It ships because operators ask for it,
it carries `T_c` and `T_elapsed` with it, and the word *projected* is not
optional in the label.

### 7a. Peak is C-weighted, is its own criterion, and the three 140s are not the same number

**Decision.** The logged peak is `L_Cpeak`. It is reported and compared **outside
the dose integral**, and the report prints the weighting in the name.

Why C: IEC 61672-1 clause 3.9 defines peak sound level for any weighting, but
ISO 1996-1 clause 3.1.4 Note 3 states that **IEC 61672-1 specifies the accuracy
of a peak detector only for C-weighting** (its clause 5.13 and Table 5). So
`L_Zpeak` and `L_Apeak` are computable and unspecified, and `L_Cpeak` is the only
peak this project can report with a standards reference behind it. IEC 61252
Ed 2 clause 4.18 independently makes C-weighted peak a **mandatory** indication
for a personal sound exposure meter while leaving dose optional and parametric —
which is the clearest possible statement that peak is a separate criterion.

**The trap, and it is the most likely correctness bug in this whole feature.**
Note before the table what the OSHA row is *not*: an earlier draft of this
record called it "unweighted peak", which reads as a positive statement that Z
weighting is required. The regulation says no such thing — it specifies no
weighting, and it restricts the limit to impulsive or impact noise. "Specifies
none" and "specifies Z" are different facts, and only the first one is in the
CFR.

| framework | the rule | what "140" actually means |
|---|---|---|
| OSHA 29 CFR 1910.95, Table G-16 note | *"Exposure to **impulsive or impact noise** should not exceed 140 dB peak sound pressure level"* | a **peak** limit that **names no weighting at all**, and applies only to impulsive or impact noise — not a ceiling on continuous level |
| EU Directive 2003/10/EC Art. 3 | `p_peak` in its own column at each of three tiers; exceeding *either* quantity trips that tier | **140 / 137 / 135 dB(C) peak** |
| NIOSH 98-126 §1.1.4 "Ceiling Limit", p.4 | *"Exposure to continuous, varying, intermittent, or impulsive noise shall not exceed 140 dBA."* — the whole of the section, one sentence, no footnote | an **A-weighted level** ceiling, not a peak at all, and it covers impulsive noise explicitly |

Three different measurements sharing one number — and one of the three does not
even constrain the same *kind* of event, since OSHA's applies to impulsive or
impact noise while the EU's and NIOSH's apply to all of it. A single "140 dB
peak" readout is silently wrong in at least two of the three, so the app names
the quantity
(`L_Cpeak`, `L_Zpeak`, `L_AFmax`) at every place it is shown and never prints a
bare "peak". *(An earlier draft carried this as unresolved — "140 dBA" against "140 dB peak
SPL for impulsive noise" — and a later one blamed the second wording on a
summary. **Both were wrong, and the primary is more interesting than either.**
NIOSH's own normative limit is unambiguous: §1.1.4, printed p.4, "140 dBA" and
nothing else. But "140 dB peak SPL" appears in the document **three times**, in
Chapter 3's discussion, as the limit NIOSH is departing from: p.17, "for
impulsive noise, the generally accepted limit… is a peak level of 140 dB SPL";
**§3.2, p.24**, "the generally accepted ceiling limit of 140 dB peak SPL for
impulsive noise is based on a report by Kryter et al. [1966]"; and **§3.4,
p.29**, quoting OSHA's own wording — "Exposure to impulsive or impact noise
should not exceed 140 dB peak sound pressure" — and adding that "the 140-dB
limit is advisory rather than mandatory". NIOSH then states its own reasoning on
p.25: "the allowable exposure time at 140 dBA is less than 0.1 sec; thus,
140 dBA is a reasonable ceiling limit for impulsive noise." So the two wordings
are a real distinction the document itself draws — the accepted peak-SPL limit
it inherited, and the A-weighted level ceiling it chose instead — and §3.2 p.24
also says why no separate continuous-noise ceiling was needed: "with an 85-dBA
REL and a 3-dB exchange rate, an exposure duration of less than 28 sec would be
allowed at a 115-dBA level.")*

**Sampled peak, not true peak.** `Leq::peakDb` is `10·log10(max p²)`, the
sampled maximum. The weighting record deferred a 4× polyphase true-peak meter to
"the meter build's second commit" and it never came, so the inter-sample maximum
of a band-limited signal can exceed what is logged. The readout says `sampled`,
and §12 carries the deferral rather than hiding it.

## 8. Decision: calibration is a flow, not a stored number, and the 0.5 dB rule is the only published criterion available

**Decision.** L6a builds the calibration flow. It is not in the L6a row of the
master plan, and it is a prerequisite rather than a feature: an SPL log in dBFS
is not an SPL log, and `Trace::calibrationOffsetDb` exists today with nothing
that can honestly set it.

**The flow.** Apply an IEC 60942 calibrator (nominal 94.0 or 114.0 dB at
1 kHz), let it settle, measure Z-weighted `L_meas` through the same `Leq` path
the session will use, and set `referenceOffsetDb = L_cal − L_meas`. Record the
offset, the calibrator's nominal level, and the time. **At session end, repeat
without adjusting** and record the second reading; the difference is the drift.

**Closed-form acceptance, no microphone.** Feed a 1 kHz sine of amplitude `A`
into the Z path: `L_meas = 10·log10(A²/2) = 20·log10(A) − 3.0102999566398120`,
so `offset = L_cal − 20·log10(A) + 3.0102999566398120`, and after applying it
`leqDb()` of the same signal must read `L_cal`. That is an identity, not a
golden vector.

**The 0.5 dB rule, and what is actually known about it.** ISO 1996-2:2017
clause 5.2 — read in full from the standard's own free preview — requires a
class 1 IEC 60942 calibrator check **at the beginning and at the end of every
measurement**, requires the difference between two consecutive checks with no
adjustment between them to be **≤ 0.5 dB**, and requires that **all results
since the previous satisfactory check be discarded** if it is exceeded. That is
the only published number for this anywhere in the research pass, it comes from
a normative clause, and it is what the app compares the drift against. (A widely
repeated identical 0.5 dB rule attributed to ISO 9612 could not be confirmed
against the ISO text and is not relied on.)

**Argued against the obvious: keep storing a bare offset and let the operator
type it.** That is what the repo does now and it is what Smaart does — Smaart's
report field list is exhaustive and contains **no calibration entry**, and
10EaZy's report does not carry a before/after pair either. So shipping the pair
is not parity, it is a differentiator; and it is the kind of differentiator this
project already chose once, when it decided to publish the weighting error table
instead of claiming a Class. 10EaZy does publish one hard number that a typed
offset cannot enforce — it refuses a calibration that "differs more than
± 1.5 dB from the factory calibration" — which is the shape of check only a flow
can perform.

**What the drift does.** It does **not** silently invalidate the log. It sets
`flags.calibrationInvalid` on the session, prints in the report with the two
readings and the clause, and the operator decides. An instrument that throws
away eight hours of evidence on its own authority is worse than one that says
why the evidence is doubtful.

## 9. Decision: one HTML document, two transports — the report is the viewer with the data frozen into it

**Decision.** There is exactly one renderer. It takes a report payload and emits
a **self-contained HTML document** (inline CSS, inline SVG, no external
assets). The **report** embeds a frozen payload; the **web viewer** serves the
same document with the payload fetched from the read-only API. "PDF" is the
browser's print-to-PDF of that document.

**Why not a PDF library.** JUCE 9.0.1 has no PDF writer — the only `pdf` matches
under `juce_graphics` are incidental strings in vendored libraries. So a real
`.pdf` means a new third-party dependency, a licence review against
AGPL-3.0-or-later, a second renderer that will drift from the on-screen one, and
a font-embedding problem the project has already solved once for JUCE and would
have to solve again. The cost of the HTML route is one real thing: the operator
must press print. The benefit is that the archived artefact is a single file
that is diffable, hashable, greppable and openable on any machine in twenty
years, and that the compliance viewer and the compliance report can never
disagree, because they are the same file.

**Why this does not create a second HTTP surface — and who now owns that
decision.** The owner ruled 2026-09-06 (`docs/UPGRADE-BACKLOG.md`,
`docs/HUMAN-QA-QUEUE.md:68`) that the remote API ships **read-only, bound to
localhost by default**. Since this record's first draft, that lane has been
written and **merged**: `docs/dsp/2026-09-16-remote-api.md`, PR #11 at
`a39a02e` (2026-09-17), and **its §12 rules on this question directly** — "L6a's SPL logging/history/alarms/web viewer (G7) is a
**client of this API**, served from **this** server, on **this** port, behind
**this** `Host` check, rate limit and token. It must not open a second socket, a
second port, a second bind default or a second auth model." Its precedent is
Smaart, whose SPL Web Viewer is plain HTTP on the **same port 26000** as its
API with the same optional password — one surface, two representations — and
its counter-example is SysTune, which bundled a whole NGINX.

Three consequences for this record, none of which it may re-decide:

- **The transport is not ours.** That record chose HTTP/1.1 + JSON over
  **cpp-httplib (MIT)**, polling with a version token, one API thread reading
  the same published pointer the UI reads, and a `Host`-header allowlist that
  outranks the bind. An earlier draft of this section reasoned from
  `juce::StreamingSocket::createListener`; that is superseded, and the viewer
  adds **no dependency beyond the one that record already took**.
- **The static page rides the same server**, from the same origin, so it is
  same-origin with the API it fetches, needs no CORS headers at all, and passes
  the `Host` check by construction (that record's §12.3).
- **The viewer must apply the same rounding the desktop UI applies** — whole
  hertz, one decimal of dB, two decimals of coherence — because the wire
  carries full precision and the same measurement must not read two ways on two
  screens (that record's §12.2, and `CLAUDE.md`). **Since PR #11 merged this is
  an obligation with a name on it, not a note**, and the names are not the ones
  an implementer would guess. **The formatters already exist and no new one may
  be written** — that record's §11 item 13 says so in those words. In
  `app/src/view/Readouts.h` (namespace `rta::view`):

  | the rule | the function that already implements it |
  |---|---|
  | whole hertz | **`formatHz`** — `Readouts.h:72`, `"{} Hz"` on `std::llround` |
  | one decimal of dB | **`formatTrim`** — `Readouts.h:79`, `"{:.1f} dB"` |
  | two decimals, 0..1 (coherence *and* `phaseAgreement`) | **`formatAgreement`** — `Readouts.h:87`, `"{:.2f}"` |

  An earlier draft of this record named `formatHz` / `formatDb` /
  `formatCoherence`. **Two of those three do not exist**, and writing them would
  have been exactly the second rounding rule §11 item 13 forbids. Note also that
  the Hz and dB formatters **return the unit inside the string**, so an
  assertion that omits the suffix fails on the suffix rather than on the
  rounding.

  Nothing about this needs new infrastructure: `Readouts.h` is already a named
  entry in `measure_has_no_framework_deps`'s `GLOBS`
  (`app/tests/CMakeLists.txt:282`), so it is framework-free and testable with
  `RTA_BUILD_APP=OFF`; the three are already pinned by
  `app/tests/test_readouts.cpp:100-115`; and `rta::view::formatHz` already has a
  live caller at `app/src/view/DevicePanel.cpp:102`. §12.2 assigns the
  *JavaScript* half to **this lane**: the viewer **reuses these three** — it
  reimplements their behaviour in JS and asserts it against the same golden
  values the C++ side uses — **or records the constraint as untested for the
  viewer**. That record's own words for why: "Two records asserting it and
  neither testing it is the failure mode this note exists to prevent." This
  record accepts the obligation and discharges it the first way; §13 Q8's answer
  decides only *when*, never whether. If the viewer is cut from L6a, the
  constraint is recorded as untested and that sentence goes in the report.

**And it hands this lane a blocker that is ours to clear.** Its §12.1 states
that `Snapshot` carries dBFS only and `rta::meter::Leq` has no `app/` caller, so
the `"spl"` entry in `/api/v1/status`'s `available` list "appears when the
Meters track lands it, and L6a is blocked on that, not on this record."
**That publish path is §1.1 of this record, and designing it is this lane's
first wave** — §2's block, §11's `core`/`app` split and the §C2 dB-convention
seam are exactly what has to exist before `"spl"` can appear in that list. The
web viewer is therefore the *last* thing L6a builds, not the first.

One question that record explicitly leaves to this lane (its §12.4): whether a
page served *from* `127.0.0.1` fetching `127.0.0.1` is exempt from Chrome's
Local Network Access prompt. It follows from LNA's same-address-space model and
was not found stated verbatim. It is an afternoon's test against Chrome 142+ and
a precondition for the viewer — **§14 carries it as UNVERIFIED and §13 Q8 asks
whether the viewer ships before that test is run.**

**The minimum payload, from what the two shipping viewers actually draw:**

```
snapshot   { t_iso, metrics[ {id,label,weighting,detector,window,valueDb} ],
             leqBufferFill 0..1, overload, blockIndex }
history    [ {blockIndex, valueDb} ] per metric  +  markers[ {blockIndex,kind,text} ]
alarms     [ {metricId, limitDb, window, state, sinceBlock, headroomDb} ]
```

Two details in there are load-bearing and are the ones a naive implementation
drops. **`leqBufferFill`** — Smaart draws it as a bar that is yellow while the
Leq buffer is partially full and green when it is full, and 10EaZy will not
issue warnings at all until a full Leq period has elapsed; a live Leq shown
without saying that its window is not yet full is a number that is quietly
wrong. And **`state` is server-computed**: the page must never re-derive an
alarm by comparing `valueDb` to `limitDb`, or the decision lives in two places
and the page and the log will eventually disagree.

**Gate the surface on logging, following Smaart** ("Logging must be running for
the API to be enabled"). A remote surface that exists when nothing is being
recorded is attack surface with no user.

**What the report must contain.** IEC 61672-1 has **no measurement-report
clause** — its documentation clauses (8 Marking, 9 Instruction Manual) bind the
manufacturer, not the measurement. The reporting clause is **ISO 1996-2:2017
clause 13, "Information to be recorded and reported"**, whose *body is
paywalled*. So the content list below is assembled from what the market
demonstrably prints plus what this record's own decisions make checkable, and it
is explicitly **not** claimed to be ISO 1996-2 clause 13 conformant:

1. **Identification** — venue, event, operator, company, engineer, production
   company, free-text notes. (Smaart SPL's exact field set, v9.1 pp. 37–38.)
2. **Instrument** — application name and version, build, device and channel,
   sample rate, and the honesty statement of §11.
3. **Calibration** — pre-check value and time, post-check value and time, the
   drift, the calibrator's nominal level, and the ISO 1996-2 clause 5.2
   criterion the drift was compared against. *Nobody in the live-sound market
   prints this; see §8.*
4. **Settings** — frequency weighting, time weighting, block interval, alarm
   windows and limits, dose preset with `L_c`, `T_c`, `q` and threshold.
5. **Results per configured metric** — Leq over the reporting period, Lmax,
   Lmin, Lpeak, SEL, and the Ln set with full ISO 1996-1 labels.
6. **Dose** — `D %`, projected `D %` with its two times, `L_EX,8h`, and the
   preset that produced them.
7. **Time history** — the strip, drawn from blocks, with alarm and overload
   markers on it.
8. **Validity** — count and total duration of overload, under-range, dropped
   samples and gaps; the segment list; and the count of blocks excluded.
9. **Integrity** — a hash over the log segments, printed in the report.

Item 9 is 10EaZy's idea and is worth copying wholesale: it writes a checksum to
the bottom of the log and ships a separate *Log File Validator* so an altered
file fails the check. For an artefact whose whole purpose is to be believed by
somebody who was not there, tamper-evidence is worth more than another metric.

## 10. Decision: the CSV is the log, the JSON is the same fields, and the header is `key=value` comment lines

**Decision.** The log file is line-oriented text, append-only:

```
# schema=1
# startedAtUnixMs=1789…      sampleRate=48000      blockSamples=48000
# weighting=A                detector=fast         calibrationOffsetDb=94.0
# calibrationUnit=dbspl      calibratorLevelDb=94.0
blockIndex,sumSquares,leqDb,maxFastDb,maxSlowDb,peakCDb,flags
0,1.2345e+04,…
```

Three properties, each chosen against an alternative:

**Append-only beats rewrite-an-index.** `SessionStore` writes its index to
`.tmp` and renames, which is right for a document that is rewritten. A log is
never rewritten; it only grows. An interrupted append leaves at most a truncated
final line, and **the reader discards it and reports how many bytes it
discarded** — no atomicity claim is made, because none can be made portably, and
the property that matters is stated as tolerance rather than as a guarantee.
This is also what Smaart does not do: it holds the log in a temp file and adds
the headers on stop, so an interrupted Smaart session leaves a file Smaart
cannot read (v9.1 p. 37).

**Settings live in the header, not in every row.** They cannot change within a
log: a weighting change starts a **new** log. The alternative — a weighting
column — makes every row independently interpretable and makes the file a
record of an instrument that was reconfigured mid-measurement, which is the
thing ISO 1996-2 clause 5.2's discard rule exists to prevent.

**`sumSquares` is in the file next to `leqDb`.** The redundancy is deliberate:
`leqDb` is what a human reads, `sumSquares` is what §3's energy sum needs to
recompute any window exactly, and a reader that has only the rounded dB cannot
reproduce the report's own numbers. JSON export carries the identical field
names, so there is one schema with two encodings, not two schemas.

**The unit of the log is one channel.** A multichannel session writes one file
per logged channel plus one session header. This falls out of L6b's routing
work and avoids inventing a wide-format CSV whose column count depends on the
interface.

## 11. Decision: what is `core/` and what is `app/`, and the one honesty rule that has to move

**`core/` — pure, closed-form, no clock, no file, no policy:**

| type | what it is | why core |
|---|---|---|
| `meter::LevelHistogram` | fixed-bin accumulator, §5, with out-of-span counters and absence | the half-bin bound is a theorem |
| `meter::BlockAccumulator` | sum of squares + detector maxima + peak over a sample count, §2 | arithmetic over a span of floats |
| `meter::combineBlocks` | the energy sum of §3 over a span of blocks | the identity `test_leq.cpp` already pins |
| `meter::headroomDb` | §6's `L_allow`, pure, no state | a closed form with exact check values |
| `meter::AlarmLatch` | the two-state latch over a windowed value | the `dsp::OverloadDetector` precedent: a pure run-length state machine already lives in `core/` |
| `meter::Dose` | §7's accumulator, `(L_c, T_c, q, threshold)` | five exact identities |
| `meter::calibrationOffsetDb` | `L_cal − L_meas`, §8 | one line, one identity, and it belongs beside `Leq` |

**`app/` — clocks, files, sockets, policy, pixels:** the ring and its span
setting, the drain-path wiring, the wall clock, the log writer and its rotation,
the CSV/JSON/HTML renderers, the calibration UI and its session state, the alarm
configuration, the history pane (a new `PaneView::Spl` in
`view/PaneRegistry.h`, which already reports `fellBack` for an unknown name),
and the API handler.

**The one rule that has to move.** `core_makes_no_class_1_claim` globs
`core/include/rta/meter/*.h`, `core/src/meter/*.cpp`, `dsp/Weighting.*` and
three named test files — so L6a's new `core/meter` files are covered for free.
**But the report is written by `app/`**, and a generated compliance report is
the single most likely place in this entire codebase for the words "IEC 61672-1
Class 1" to appear, and the most damaging. The guard's scope must grow to cover
the report and export templates, or the honesty rule that the weighting record
established stops being enforced exactly where it matters. The report instead
prints the honest sentence the project has used since 2026-08-27: analytic
weighting within 0.05 dB of Table 3, digital filter error as published, **no
class claim**, and — new here — that the instrument's linear operating range,
overload and under-range behaviour have not been verified against IEC 61672-1
clause 3.28's validity definition.

Second guard note: `measure_has_no_framework_deps` is an explicit ~65-entry file
list, not a glob, and `memory/core-must-not-include-frameworks.md` records that
a misspelled entry silently reduces coverage. Every JUCE-free file L6a adds must
be appended by hand and the guard's own `OK (N files scanned)` count is the only
proof it was.

## 13. Open questions for a human

1. **The 3.0103 dB seam (§C2 of the research).** `Levels.h` makes a full-scale
   sine read `0.0 dBFS`; `meter::Leq` makes it read `−3.0103`. Both are right
   for what they answer. Does the broadband SPL readout adopt the mean-square
   convention and sit beside sine-referenced RTA bands (the two numbers differ
   by 3.0103 dB until calibration, and the label says which), or do the bands
   move? **Moving the bands changes every dBFS number already on screen and in
   every stored trace**, so the default proposed here is: convert once at the
   meter seam, label both, change nothing that exists.

2. **Build the calibration flow inside L6a (§8), which the L6a row does not
   list.** Without it there is a stored `calibrationOffsetDb` that nothing can
   honestly set, and no report can carry the ISO 1996-2 clause 5.2 start/end
   pair. The proposal is to build it; it is a scope addition and needs the
   owner's word.

3. **Which Ln set ships, and how many are user-settable.** Larson Davis exposes
   **six** with settable percentages; Smaart's report prints **L10/L50/L90**;
   NoiseCapture implements only those three. Proposal: six slots, defaulting to
   L1/L5/L10/L50/L90/L95.

4. **Which NIOSH convention ships (§7).** 98-126's **Table 1-1** and the
   formula printed above it need `q = 3/log10(2) = 9.9657843`; its **Table 1-2**
   and the footnote printed under it need `q = 10`. Two tables, one chapter,
   different exchange constants, and `q = 10` reads up to **4.2549 % low** at
   140 dB(A), the top of NIOSH's own measurement range. Proposal: reproduce
   Table 1-1, print the TWA with NIOSH's own formula, and state the difference
   in the report. Related and cheap to decide at the same time: 98-126 contains
   **three arithmetic errata** (§7) — does the app reproduce NIOSH's published
   tables *including* the errata, or its formulas? They are different products.

5. **Is the alarm window sliding or consecutive-fixed?** Both fall out of §3's
   blocks. Sliding is strictly more conservative (its maximum is ≥ the fixed
   one's). VLAREM registers `LAeq,60min` and deems it satisfied by
   `LAeq,15min ≤ 102`; the Pop Code monitors 1-minute LAeq against a 15-minute
   limit. Proposal: sliding, with the regulated window and the proxy window both
   shown.

6. **Does the log need to be tamper-evident?** 10EaZy writes a checksum and
   ships a separate Log File Validator; Cirrus keeps a parallel secure store.
   §9 item 9 proposes a hash in the report. A *signature* needs a key and a
   story about where the key lives — out of scope until asked.

7. **Retention and rotation.** VLAREM requires registered data kept **at least
   one month**; the Swiss cantonal rules say **six months**. What is the default
   log span (§4), what is the segment size, and does the app ever delete?

8. **Does the web viewer ship in L6a at all?** It is last in the build order
   (§9); PR #11 has now landed, so what remains is an unrun Chrome
   Local-Network-Access test. It is the cleanest thing to cut if the lane is
   too big.

9. **Buy ISO 1996-2:2017?** Clause 13 "Information to be recorded and reported"
   is *the* reporting clause for a logging meter and its body is paywalled;
   clauses 10.2.2 (L_N,T) and 10.3 (incomplete or corrupted data) are the other
   two this lane would build against. Unlike ISO 2969 and IEC 60268-16 it does
   **not block** the lane — §9's report list is assembled from market practice
   and is labelled as not claiming clause-13 conformance — but it would turn an
   assembled list into a cited one.

10. **The weighting record's "Table 2" (§1.2).** The correction is a
    documentation fix in `docs/dsp/2026-08-27-weighting-and-meters.md` and in
    two comment lines of `core/tests/check_no_conformance_claim.cmake`. This
    docs-only PR amends the record; the guard's comment is code. Who takes it?

## 15. Amendment 2026-09-18: what station 4 Wave 0 measured and changed

*Added at the end of L6a station 4 Wave 0 (branch `l6a/wave0-spl-publish`,
PR #17), after two adversarial verifier rounds on that PR. **Every paragraph
above is left standing**: this section records where the built code differs
from what the decision was made from, and why, so a reader who acts on §2 or
§3 alone is pointed here rather than being silently contradicted. Inline
pointers sit at the two places that matter (§2's payload table, §3's
"retire a block" paragraph).*

### A1 — §2's block payload gained one field and one flag

The field list in §2 is 36 bytes of payload. As laid out, the `double` forces
four bytes of padding after the leading `uint64 + uint32`, so the struct was
always 40 bytes with four of them wasted. `droppedSamples` occupies **exactly
that padding**:

```
blockIndex        uint64    offset 0     the clock; no wall time in any arithmetic
blockSamples      uint32    offset 8     the divisor every mean in this block used
droppedSamples    uint32    offset 12    NEW -- samples the bus LOST before this
                                         block closed, in the former padding
sumSquares        double    offset 16    the energy
maxFastDb         float     offset 24    MAX of the F detector WITHIN the block
maxSlowDb         float     offset 28    MAX of the S detector WITHIN the block
peakDb            float     offset 32    C-weighted sampled peak WITHIN the block
flags             uint32    offset 36    BlockFlag bitmask
                                         sizeof == 40, alignof == 8
```

Measured, not assumed: three `static_assert`s in
`core/include/rta/meter/Block.h` (a disagreeing compiler fails the **build**,
not a test) and the offsets printed by `core/tests/test_block.cpp`'s A1 case.
**§4's ring-size table therefore does not move** — that was the condition on
adding the field at all.

`flags` gains a fifth bit, `Gap`. It is set when the paired drain stalled and
the bus lost samples, and `droppedSamples` says by how much. The two ship
together on purpose: the `Gap` bit alone is unrecoverable from the log, because
the live drop counter lives in memory and never reaches the file — so a log
carrying only the bit would admit that time was lost and be unable to say how
much, and every timestamp reconstructed after the first drop would be
permanently early for the rest of an eight-hour session. With the count, a third
party holding only the text reconstructs the true sample position of every block
as `Σ(blockSamples + droppedSamples)`, in integers, exactly. This is SPL-R1 ∧
SPL-R2 of the station-3 plan, and `test_block.cpp` A8 is the fixture.

### A2 — which flags retire a block from a window: `CalibrationInvalid` alone

§3 says "an overload latch or a `calibrationInvalid` verdict can retire a
block". The shipped rule is narrower, and it is stated in
`core/include/rta/meter/Block.h` beside `excludesFromWindow` rather than left
to each call site, because **it changes every published Leq during a loud
show**:

| flag | membership | why |
|---|---|---|
| `CalibrationInvalid` | **EXCLUDES** | its dB are referenced to an offset ISO 1996-2:2017 cl. 5.2's own discard rule says must not be trusted. The **one** published normative criterion this lane has. |
| `Overload` | **INCLUDES** | a clipped waveform carries **less** energy than the signal that clipped it, so dropping the block *removes the loudest moment of the show* and biases the compliance number **in the operator's favour** — the same failure §2 already rejects when it refuses instantaneous sampling. |
| `UnderRange` | **INCLUDES** | excluding floor readings biases Leq **up**. |
| `Dropped`, `Gap` | **INCLUDE** | the energy they carry is real over the samples that existed, and `blockSamples` is the honest divisor for it. What was lost is **time**, and that is what `droppedSamples` records (A1). |

All five are **counted and reported** either way (§9 item 8):
`WindowResult` carries `excludedBlocks`, `overloadBlocks`, `underRangeBlocks`,
`droppedBlocks`, `gapBlocks` and `droppedSamplesTotal`. Five fixtures pin it
(`test_block.cpp` A9–A13), and the mutation "make `Overload` exclude" is red.

**What is NOT claimed.** IEC 61672-1 cl. 3.28's validity definition and
ISO 1996-2 cl. 10.3 ("incomplete or corrupted data") are **paywalled and
unread**. This project therefore claims **no standard basis** for the four
inclusions — only for the one exclusion, which has cl. 5.2 behind it. The four
are engineering judgement with the bias direction argued above, and they are
carried as owner question **Q11** in `docs/HUMAN-QA-QUEUE.md`. `UnderRange` in
particular has **no criterion anywhere in the shipped code**: cl. 5.12 is
paywalled, so `core/` holds no threshold for it and the flag is a caller's to
set. It is reserved, not implemented.

### A3 — the overload run is read off the RAW hop, and IEC is not the authority for it

**The finding.** The overload latch cannot sit downstream of a weighting
filter. Every sample that reaches the block accumulator has already been
through A, C or Z, and **measured on this branch**: the C-weighted copy of
three consecutive samples at `rta::dsp::kFullScaleThreshold` **does not reach
that threshold at all**, so the block reads clean. A run detected on a
weighted stream is a run in a signal the converter never delivered. The latch
therefore reads the **raw** hop, in `rta::measure::SplMeter`, and hands the
resulting flag to `core` through `setFlag`; `core` holds no overload threshold
of its own. The station-3 plan's SPL-R4 already placed the latch in `SplMeter`
for a different reason (a run must survive a block boundary, which
`rta::dsp::hasOverload`'s own header says it cannot do across calls) — this is
a second, independent reason for the same placement, and `app/tests/
test_spl_meter.cpp` B3 was **red** against an implementation that used the
C-weighted stream.

**On what authority.** Two things, and **neither is IEC 61672-1**:

1. **Smaart LE v9.1 p. 78**, for the criterion itself — three or more
   consecutive samples at or above full scale. That is a published vendor
   criterion, quoted in `core/include/rta/dsp/OverloadDetector.h`, and this
   lane reuses the shipped constant rather than restating a threshold.
2. **Engineering grounds**, for the choice of stream: overload is a statement
   about what the analogue-to-digital converter delivered, and a weighting
   filter changes every sample's value, so the only stream on which the
   statement is true is the one the converter produced. The measurement above
   is the evidence.

**IEC 61672-1 is explicitly NOT cited here.** Its overload and under-range
clauses — **5.11, 5.12, 5.17 and 5.18** — are **paywalled and have not been
read** by anyone on this project. Only the standard's cover, its complete
table of contents and clauses 3.1–3.31 were available (see
`docs/research/2026-09-16-l6a-spl-pro-station1-research.md` §A1). So no
conformance to those clauses is claimed, the guard
`core_makes_no_class_1_claim` exists to keep it that way, and any future
reader tempted to write "per IEC 61672-1 cl. 5.11" beside this code must buy
the clause first. (§12 already lists these four clauses under what this lane
cannot claim; this amendment names them again because the overload *placement*
is the decision most likely to attract a spurious citation.)

### A4 — three tolerances in the station-3 plan are arithmetically wrong

Recorded here because they are numbers, and the plan is where numbers of this
kind are supposed to be trustworthy. All three were **falsified by
measurement** on MSVC 14.51 x64:

| plan row | plan predicted | measured | cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| W0-A A2, `a = 1/√2` | `8.882e-16` | **`1.955e-12`** | `1/√2` rounded to float32 does not square to a value whose 48 000-term sum is exact; only `a = 1.0` does |
| W0-B B5 | 1e-9 on a full-scale **sine** | **`−8.27e-08 dB`** | float32 **sample quantisation**, not summation: half a float ULP near amplitude 1.0 is `2.98e-08` relative, so ~`2.6e-07 dB` |
| W0-E E2/E3 | 1e-9 | **`−8.27e-08 dB`** | the same |

A sine cannot reach 1e-9 through a `float` buffer on **any** toolchain, so
these are not machine differences and must not be re-widened per-CI-job. Each
shipped test states the reason and asserts the **exact** form of the same
identity beside it on an exactly representable fixture — an alternating ±1
signal, where the mean square is exactly 1.0 and the comparison is bitwise.
The plan's W0-B B2 `5τ` deficit figure (`−0.0286 dB`) is wrong too: the closed
form is `−0.02936` from silence. It did not matter only because that row says
assert the formula, never the literal — which is the rule that saved it.

### A5 — the metric list is capped, and the cap is reported

`SplConfig::metrics` is bounded by `SplConfig::kMaxMetrics` (16), truncated at
`SplSession::start`, and the number dropped is published on
`SplBlockView::refusedMetrics`. Truncation rather than refusing the session:
a misconfiguration that silenced SPL logging outright would lose a show's
evidence, which is worse than logging the first sixteen — and that is only
acceptable **because the count is reported**. Found by PR #17's verifier as a
defect: an unbounded list overflowed the publish path's fixed per-metric window
storage, and the fallback that followed published a **C-weighted metric's label
over the A chain's number**, measured at 18.843 dB wrong. §2's payload and §3's
arithmetic are untouched by this; it is recorded here because the *config
schema* is a decision the record should carry.
