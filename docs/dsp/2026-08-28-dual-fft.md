# Dual-FFT transfer-function engine — decisions

*2026-08-28. Lane L2, station 2. Written from the station-1 research pass; the
sources are cited where a decision rests on them. Nothing in `core/` implements
any of this yet — this record exists so the implementation cannot silently
choose otherwise.*

The five decisions below are the ones the research named as genuinely open. Each
records what the rejected option would have cost, because a decision without its
reasoning is a decision the next session reverses by accident.

---

## 1. Estimator: keep three accumulators, default to H1, ship the gap

**Decision.** The engine accumulates `Sxx`, `Syy`, `Sxy` and nothing else.
`H1 = Sxy/Sxx` is the default output. `H2 = Syy/Syx` and the total-least-squares
`Hv` are computed from those same accumulators on demand. The H1/H2 gap is
exposed, not hidden.

**Why H1 is the default.** H1 is unbiased when the noise is on the *output*
channel. That is the live-sound case: the reference is tapped electrically and
the measurement mic sits in a room full of uncorrelated energy — crowd, HVAC,
other sources, air handling. H2 assumes the opposite and would bias high by
`(1 + Gmm/Gyy)`.

**The argument against it, which must be recorded.** The obvious objection is
not theoretical, it is empirical: **Open Sound Meter, the closest open-source
competitor, does not use H1 at all.** It computes a per-frame magnitude ratio
`|B|/|A|` and a per-frame phase difference, then averages magnitude and phase
separately (`src/source/measurement.cpp`). If the nearest comparable product
took a different road, "H1 because Bendat says so" is not yet an argument.

It is rejected anyway, on two specific defects rather than on authority:

1. **A ratio of magnitudes is not the ratio of expectations.**
   `E[|B|/|A|] ≠ |E[Sxy]|/E[Sxx]`. Averaging a ratio biases with mic noise in a
   way the Welch-consistent form does not. Only accumulating the spectra *then*
   dividing is consistent with Welch 1967.
2. **Averaging phase as a scalar breaks at the wrap.** Two frames reading +179°
   and −179° average to 0°, which is the opposite of the truth. The defect is
   invisible except near ±180°, which is exactly where alignment work happens.

**What keeping all three buys, at no cost.** Bendat & Piersol's identity
`H1/H2 = γ²` means the three estimators validate each other for free — it
becomes a CI assertion (§6) rather than a claim. It also makes
reference-channel noise *diagnosable*: a large H1/H2 gap where coherence is high
is a statement about the measurement, not about the room.

**What none of them fix.** Late reverberation puts correlated-but-delayed energy
on the mic, and every estimator treats correlated energy as signal. No estimator
choice addresses that; only windowing does. That belongs to L3 (MTW), not here,
and the engine must not pretend otherwise.

## 2. Coherence is magnitude-**squared**, and the type says so

**Decision.** `γ² = |Gxy|² / (Gxx · Gyy)`. Named in the API as
magnitude-squared coherence, not as a bare `coherence` float.

**Why.** It is what Bendat & Piersol, SciPy and MATLAB define, so every golden
vector and every cross-check has an unambiguous reference. Decisively, the free
self-test in §6 — `H1/H2 == γ²` — holds only for the squared form.

**Recorded so nobody "fixes" it later:** Open Sound Meter displays the
**un-squared** `|Grm|/√(Grr·Gmm)` (`src/math/coherence.cpp`). Our numbers will
therefore not match OSM's on identical data; ours will read lower. That is a
different quantity, not a bug. Anyone comparing the two products side by side
will see the discrepancy and must not "correct" it.

CLAUDE.md already fixes the presentation — coherence displays 0..1 with two
decimals — and says nothing about which definition. This record fixes that half.

---

## 3. No coherence below a minimum number of averages, enforced by the type

**Decision.** Coherence is not emitted until at least **8 averaged frames** have
accumulated. Below that the snapshot carries no coherence value at all — absence
is represented in the type, not by a sentinel like `0.0` or `-1`.

**Why this is not a stylistic choice.** For a single un-averaged frame,
`|X*Y|² ≡ |X|²·|Y|²` identically, so **coherence is exactly 1.0 at every
frequency**. A completely broken engine looks flawless. MATLAB documents this
outright. The same trap returns after every `reset()` and on the first frame
after any configuration change — the three moments a developer is most likely to
be looking at the screen.

**Where the 8 comes from, and what it is not.** The estimator bias is
approximately `0.5·√(π/N)`: about 0.31 at N=8, 0.28 at N=10, 0.22 at N=16. There
is no cliff, so any threshold is a judgement, and 8 is a judgement — one power of
two, bias below a third, configurable by the caller. **It is not a standard and
must never be cited as one.** What is not a judgement is the rule that *some*
floor must exist and must be expressed in the type: returning a number that is
identically 1.0 by construction is worse than returning nothing.

## 4. Delay: GCC-PHAT, relative regularisation, sub-sample peak — and it is
applied *before* the FFT

**Decision.** The delay finder is GCC-PHAT — `Ψ = 1/|Gxy|` — with the
regularisation floor taken **relative** to `max|G|`, plus parabolic sub-sample
interpolation on the correlation peak, returning `{delaySamples, subSample,
peak, polarity}`. It is a free function: spans in, that struct out. It owns no
state.

**Why PHAT over the alternatives.** PHAT is flat-weighted, which is what makes
it robust in reverberation — the case this tool exists for. Roth weighting
(`1/Gxx`, which is what a plain `A/B` deconvolution is, as Open Sound Meter
does) biases the estimate toward frequencies where the reference happens to be
strong. On a clean electrical loopback the two agree, so the choice only shows
up in the situation that matters. REW and Smaart use the IR peak, which is Roth
with a full deconvolution — the same bias with a different interface.

**Cost of the rejected options, and of this one.** Roth is cheaper to justify
because it falls out of a deconvolution one wants anyway. PHAT's own weakness is
low-SNR narrowband content, where flattening the weighting amplifies bins that
carry nothing; the mitigation is an optional band limit on the correlation, not
a change of weighting.

**Relative, not absolute, regularisation.** Open Sound Meter floors at −140 dB
absolute; Friture uses `ε = 1e-10 · max|G|`. An absolute floor couples the
estimator to input level, so the same signal 20 dB quieter behaves differently.
Relative is correct.

**The ordering rule, which is the part that bites.** The measured delay must be
compensated on the reference **before** the transform, as an integer-sample ring
offset — never applied afterwards to the phase. A delay comparable to the frame
length decorrelates the two channels and destroys coherence **at high frequency
first**, because HF has the shortest period. Compensate after the FFT and the
coherence you display is a measurement of your own misalignment rather than of
the system.

Sub-sample interpolation is not a nicety: Friture's own source concedes that
without it, decimation caps delay resolution at roughly 3 cm.

## 5. Averaging: FIFO and exponential, counting **effective** frames

**Decision.** Two modes, both on the same accumulators: a FIFO running sum with
exact drop-off past a fixed depth, and the one-pole exponential already
implemented in `SpectrumEngine`. The reported average count is the **effective**
number of independent averages, not the number of frames pushed.

**Why the effective count matters and why nobody does it.** Harris 1978's
Table 1 carries an overlap-correlation column precisely because overlapped
segments are not independent: at 75 % overlap with a Hann window, K frames buy
far fewer than K averages' worth of variance reduction. The research found that
none of the comparable products derate for this — they all report the raw frame
count.

This is not pedantry, because §3 gates coherence on the average count. Gating on
a raw count that overstates independence would let coherence through before it
is trustworthy — the very failure §3 exists to prevent. The two decisions are
load-bearing on each other.

**Reuse, do not re-derive.** The PSD scaling — `2/(fs·Σw²)` with DC and Nyquist
halved — already exists in `SpectrumEngine.cpp` and must be shared, not
reimplemented. Two divergent scalings in one codebase is a bug that surfaces as
a constant dB offset nobody can find.

## 6. Phase unwrap and group delay: one lives in the view, one in core

**Decision.** **Unwrapping is a display operation** in the app-side view model.
It never feeds back into the engine and is never accumulated. **Group delay is
computed in core** from the closed form `τg = −Im(dH/dω · H*) / |H|²`, with its
smoothing width stated in the output, and both are gated by coherence.

**Why unwrap cannot live in the engine.** Unwrapping is ambiguous wherever
coherence is low, and one bad bin propagates a 360° step to *every bin above it*.
An ambiguous, history-dependent transform must not contaminate an accumulator
that later frames depend on. Keeping it in the view means a bad unwrap is one
bad frame on screen, not a corrupted state.

**Why group delay cannot live in the view.** It is a derivative, so it amplifies
bin-to-bin noise, which means it needs the smoothed phase and the closed form
rather than a naive difference of the displayed trace. That requires the complex
`H`, which the view should not be doing arithmetic on.

## 7. How CI proves all of this with no sound card

The `core/` no-framework rule is what makes this possible, and the shape it
forces is pull-based: the caller passes two aligned `std::span<const float>`
blocks plus an integer reference delay, and the engine returns an immutable
snapshot. Core owns no device, no clock, no thread.

The tests, in the project's order of preference:

1. **One closed-form identity pins almost everything.** Feed `y[n] = g·x[n−D]`
   from the existing synthetic generator. Then `H` must be exactly
   `g·e^{−j2πkD/N}`, coherence exactly 1.0, group delay exactly `D/fs`, and the
   GCC-PHAT peak exactly `D`. Magnitude, phase, unwrap, group delay and the
   delay finder are all pinned by a single identity with no golden file.
2. **A known biquad.** Push `x` through the existing `Biquad` and assert `H`
   matches the analytic `H(e^{jω})` from that filter's own coefficients, within
   the transform's leakage bound.
3. **Coherence against theory.** `y = x + independent noise` at a known SNR
   gives `γ² = SNR/(1+SNR)` per bin asymptotically. Assert the mean against
   that, with `0.5·√(π/N)` as the tolerance. **This is the test that catches the
   identically-1.0 bug** — the reason it is listed explicitly rather than left
   to judgement.
4. **The free bracket.** Assert `H1/H2 == γ²` numerically. It costs nothing and
   validates all three estimators against each other.
5. **Golden vectors only for what the above cannot pin** — `scipy.signal.csd`
   and `coherence` on a fixed seed, to catch scaling and normalisation drift.
   Note: the venv that can generate these lives in the **main checkout**, not in
   any worktree (`memory/build-toolchain-on-this-machine.md`).
6. **A guard test** in the spirit of the existing conformance guards: fail the
   build if coherence can be obtained with fewer than the declared minimum
   averages. §3 is only real if something enforces it.

## What this record does not decide

- Multi-time-window band stitching (lane L3). §1 notes that no estimator fixes
  late reverberation; windowing does, and that decision belongs there.
- Multi-channel transfer functions and spatial averaging (L6b).
- Anything about how traces are drawn, stored or compared (L5).
